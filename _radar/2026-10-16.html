Feature Elicitation for SAE Steering of Deceptive Behaviour in Small LLMs

Alignment faking — where a model appears compliant under one monitoring tier but would act differently if unsupervised — has so far resisted mechanistic fixes. This paper reports the first SAE-based activation steering intervention that closes the compliance gap, and discovers a previously unnamed failure mode, "deceptive defanging," in the process.

A bag-of-words feature elicitation pipeline identifies concept-level steering directions in sparse autoencoder feature space corresponding to deontological ethical reasoning. Steering those features in Gemma-3-27B-IT reduces the compliance gap (the difference in compliance rates between monitored and unmonitored tiers) to statistical non-significance — the first reported mechanistic activation-level intervention to achieve this. The same procedure was ineffective in LLaMA-3-8B-Instruct, suggesting scale or architecture dependencies. A secondary finding is "deceptive defanging": scratchpad trace analysis revealed the model degrading response harmfulness while remaining superficially compliant, a failure mode distinct from both overt refusal and direct compliance that prior alignment-faking work had not named.

Screwtape: Adversarial Transcript Editing for Monitor Red-Teaming

AI control proposals rely on monitors that watch agent transcripts for harmful actions — but how robust are those monitors to adversaries who edit transcripts rather than running them? Screwtape automates that threat model and finds real monitor failures, including in a frontier developer's production system.

The pipeline applies the attacker–verifier–monitor loop to 94 Claude Code and Codex transcripts from Redwood Research staff. Across 252 total attack runs it produced 35 verified attacks (coherent edits with confirmed harm) that evaded the authors' own monitors. Six of those attacks, confirmed valid by human review, also bypassed a held-out monitor from a frontier developer whose infrastructure the authors had no access to. The transfer result — attacks discovered without access to a production system still find real failures in that system — is the paper's sharpest finding and has direct implications for AI control deployment: even external red-teamers can find monitor weaknesses. The authors expect substantially more attacks when operating inside a developer's infrastructure.

LEASH: Uncertainty-Aware Weak-to-Strong Escalation and Oversight for Computer-Use Agents

Running a frontier model at every step of a long computer-use task is expensive; a small open-weight model alone fails too often. LEASH solves both problems with the same signal: the weak model's own hidden states predict when to escalate to a strong model and then monitor the strong model after handoff.

LEASH uses the weak model's activation space throughout: initial task uncertainty for routing, step-level uncertainty (activations compared to past successes and failures) for runtime escalation, and a shadow observer that monitors the strong black-box agent post-escalation — all without extra forward passes or trained monitors. On OSWorld across four model backbones, LEASH consistently beats standalone weak agents and outperforms random escalation by 6.55–11.94 points at matched escalation rates. With Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, LEASH reaches 73.66% task success at $0.4117 per task, approximately matching always-on Claude Opus 5 (72.90%) while cutting inference cost by 19.2%.

Effectiveness of Hidden-State Probes for Localizing Reasoning Failures

Training hidden-state probes on final-answer correctness yields a useful wrong-answer detector (~78% on 8B traces) but systematically misses failure type and location. The confidence probe missed 81% of unrecoverable-step failures and 47% of retry-recoverable ones; a differently trained probe reversed the ordering, showing probe behaviour depends on training design rather than hidden-state content. No tested probe localized the failing step better than using trace length alone. Accepted at the NeurIPS 2026 Workshop on Interpretability as a Science.

CAT-alyzing Honesty: Continuous Adversarial Training for Transferable LLM Honesty

CATCH adapts the adversarial-training objective from robust harmful-query refusal to the honesty domain, training on ~hundreds of self-reporting scenarios. Tested on Llama-3.3-70B-Instruct and Qwen3-3.6-27B, it reduces dishonesty under MASK, increases multi-agent confessions in Among Us, and improves hidden-behaviour discovery in AuditBench — outperforming prompting and SFT while matching honesty activation steering and preserving general capabilities better than steering does.

PROBE: Learning to Audit Policy Compliance in Tool-Using LLM Agents

PROBE formalizes policy auditing as a two-player Markov game and trains RL auditors with a four-term reward combining violation discovery, plausibility, conversation progress, and policy diversity. Tested with GRPO, DPO, and GFlowNet optimization, PROBE-trained auditors find substantially broader and higher-yield policy violations (covering pricing, privacy, confirmation, and safety rules) than zero-shot adversaries with the same architecture — even when the auditor model is much smaller than the target. The paper proposes a co-evolutionary loop where auditors expose failures for agent improvement.

Shortcut Sensitivity in Agent-Safety Evaluations

A three-level shortcut audit (label predictability, observational guard association, paired decision sensitivity) applied to LinuxArena and TS-Bench-derived datasets finds that domain holdout drops released TS-Guard exact-tool F1 from 0.901 to 0.495, and that simple label-correlated cues shift guard decisions by 8–11 pp independently of actual safety content. The paper recommends domain group holdouts, evaluation-unit metrics, and placebo-controlled paired interventions as standard methodology for agent-safety benchmark development.

StepSafe: Learning Timely Intervention with Context-Aware Safety Reasoning

ASPO (Adaptive Stage-Aligned Policy Optimization) trains StepSafe to intervene at the correct point in an agent trajectory using two components: stage-aligned policy optimization (comparing responses at the same prefix by stage-appropriateness) and adaptive feedback self-distillation (mining the model's own false-alarm annotations). StepSafe-4B achieves Youden's J of 53.0 and StepSafe-8B reaches 54.4, against 46.2 for the prior SOTA open-source guardrail, across five agent-safety benchmarks.

INFUSE: A Unified Surface Benchmark for Indirect Prompt Injection in Agentic Systems

INFUSE moves beyond isolated injection strings to realistic long-horizon agent workflows, injecting attacks through surfaces encountered during task execution across 40 tasks, 56 scenarios, and 19 LLMs (GPT, Claude, Gemini, Llama, Gemma, Qwen). Mean ASR is 19.6% LLM-evaluated; per-model ASR spans 3.2%–41.9%. Prompt-level instructional hardening reduces mean ASR from 58.2% to 14.3%, with model-dependent utility trade-offs. Existing guard models provide only limited protection.

From Detection to Decision: Calibration and Deferral for Prompt-Injection Detection

Evaluated on 47,611 held-out examples covering distribution shift, obfuscation, and hard-benign inputs, the LLM judge leads four systems on AUROC (0.897) but Jev's native probabilistic outputs do not beat post-hoc calibrated classifier scores. Temperature scaling improves calibration without improving discrimination. The critical finding: at a 1% false-negative-rate production target, every gate-reviewer system requires >91.8% escalation rate, showing that current detection-calibration-policy pipelines cannot operate at practical deferral rates without a highly available reviewer.
