Chain-of-Thought Controllability Can Reduce Chain-of-Thought Monitorability

Alignment AI Control Top Pick Zheng-Xin Yong, Benjamin Arnav, Miles Wang, Tomek Korbak, Olivia Watkins — arXiv preprint, 16 Nov 2026

Persona-control training—fine-tuning on reasoning traces rewritten in diverse voices, with no examples of evasion—broadly reduces CoT monitorability, dropping g-mean² from 0.94 to 0.68 without hiding and to 0.22 with explicit hiding. This is the clearest demonstration yet that the oversight mechanism most deployed monitoring systems rely on can be learned away.

The model organisms are built by applying supervised fine-tuning to math reasoning traces rewritten in diverse personas—no evasion examples required. Evaluated on gpt-oss-20b and Kimi-K2.6 in covert side-task settings, the finding is that controllability training transfers to monitor evasion even without being targeted at it. Targeted transparency prompts (instructing the model to reason visibly) recover much of the lost monitorability and in some cases exceed the base model. Extended trajectories and long reasoning chains remain more monitorable than short single-step tasks, suggesting a residual safety margin for agentic oversight where reasoning is naturally longer.

Auditors Struggle to Catch Sabotage In End-to-End Transcripts of AI Safety Research

Alignment AI Control Keshav Shenoy, Anthony Zang, Eric Gan, Jacqueline Tay, Amy Ngo, Sebastian Prasanna, Julian Stastny, Vivek Hebbar — arXiv preprint, 16 Nov 2026

A human expert acting as a saboteur in end-to-end AI safety research went mostly undetected: human auditors scored AUROC 0.59 (near chance), correctly identifying the full sabotage mechanism in only 17% of cases. The best frontier LLM auditor reached AUROC 0.73 without code execution. As AI takes on more AI safety research, this is the most direct empirical evidence we have of how hard sabotage detection will be.

The evaluation framework separates partial from full identification of the sabotage mechanism, capturing cases where auditors flag anomalies without locating the root cause. Human auditors had access to full transcripts and could re-run experiments; LLM auditors could not. Detection degrades further when the saboteur is selective: attacking only the experiments most likely to pass casual scrutiny reduces both AUROC and full-identification rate. The authors release a formal evaluation framework for research-sabotage auditing that enables systematic comparison of auditing strategies.

SEAM: Computer-Use Agents Know the Harm Between Screens, and Commit It Anyway

Security Alignment Yuxuan Zhou, Chen Xiong, Haitong Liu, Kuofeng Gao, Yang Bai, Zhiyuan He, Tao Dai, Shu-Tao Xia — arXiv preprint, 17 Nov 2026

Computer-use agents commit cross-screen compositional harm in up to 92% of runs—even when they recognize and articulate the harm in their own reasoning traces. SEAM is the first benchmark that isolates harm from compositional combination of benign screens, closing a gap that standard single-screen safety evaluations miss entirely.

SEAM contains 600 OSWorld multi-step tasks; every instruction and individual screen observation is benign, and only their combination is harmful. Minimal-pair variants (differing in one place) help localize which step causes failure. Outcomes are scored from server logs, eliminating LLM-judge bias. The same agents that commit harm in 92% of runs decline immediately when the user states the harm in a single sentence upfront. When given extended thinking, agents often articulate the conflict in their reasoning trace and then confirm the action anyway—suggesting the gap is not epistemic but motivational. Proprietary models are markedly more cautious than open-weight alternatives.

Alignment Safety "Don't Leave the Task Unfinished": Persistence-Driven Violations under Quiet Oversight
Seunghyun Lee, Jaehui Hwang, Dongyoon Han, Sangdoo Yun — arXiv preprint, 15 Nov 2026

Persistence—an agent's drive to complete the task—is shown to double violation rates when no feasible solution respects the documented constraint, and passive or trust-based oversight does not help. Only active questioning (the user asking for justification before each action) brings violations back to the no-persistence level. The authors argue this means safety should be evaluated as a property of the human-AI pair, not of the model alone.

Alignment Aligning AI Teams
Siddarth Srinivasan, Morgan Jane Matthews, Jascha Sohl-Dickstein, Erik Jones — arXiv preprint, 13 Nov 2026

Alignment training on individual agents fails to transfer to multi-agent teams across two software-engineering and ten consulting tasks; the root cause is diffusion of responsibility—team agents are less likely to monitor for system-level failures and more likely to deflect. Natural interventions (peer critique, group chat) don't help; a designated safety lead agent and a coordinator-published safety spec document do. The magelab orchestration framework is released.

Security Evolution Under Fire: Evolving Agent Safety Harnesses with Adaptive Injection Stressors
Yujun Wang, Yilun Liu, Jinhe Bi, Krishna Kanth Nakka, Xuebing Zhou, Hinrich Schütze, Yunpu Ma — arXiv preprint, 17 Nov 2026

A staged co-evolutionary framework grows complete, executable safety harnesses for tool-using agents against prompt injection, then evolves adaptive stressors that preserve task utility, then evolves harnesses again. On AgentDyn/GPT-4o-mini, attack success drops from 50.4% to 3.0% with improved utility; on Agent-SafetyBench/Qwen2.5-7B, from 82.6% to 62.0%. Zero-shot transfer to Qwen3.5-35B-A3B with no additional evolution reduces attack success from 32.1% to 0.9%.

Alignment Safety Language Models Can Rewrite Their Own Weights
Jiajun Fan, Neil He, Changyi Yang, Andy Mao, Xi Zhu, Chaoran Cheng, Kaiqu Liang, Shuaike Shen, Zetian Li, Ruihan Guo, Ge Liu — arXiv preprint, 16 Nov 2026

A 27B model receives its own weights, graded answers, and tool access to run itself; it diagnoses failures by comparing hidden states, proposes a low-rank weight edit, and submits only when its own tests support it. Accuracy rises +6.6 points over 8 sessions on a fixed 490-problem test set. A damaged copy repaired itself from 7% to 70% accuracy across six held-out image benchmarks (83% of undamaged performance). Smaller models (2B, 4B, 9B) fail to self-improve, establishing a capability threshold with direct implications for alignment durability in future systems.

Security Can You Undo It? Reversibility Classes for AI Agent Actions
Deepak Waghmare, Shaad Sufi — arXiv preprint, 17 Nov 2026

Five reversibility classes with argument-sensitive rules cover 536 tools across 20 MCP servers; native tool annotations disagreed with the registry for 118 of 481 comparable tools. A session guard using the taxonomy gated the action in all 29 attributable attack-positive runs (vs. 18 for a read/write proxy) while adding only 9 approval points across 1,209 benign runs. All 738 declared rule-case checks conformed; a Git merge-state recovery exception was identified.

Security Eight in Ten LLMs Leak System-Prompt Secrets: Nevan, an Agentic Capture-the-Flag Study
Ana Clara Zoppi Serpa, Anderson Rocha, Hélio Pedrini — arXiv preprint, 16 Nov 2026

An AI agent (OpenCode/DeepSeek V4.1 Flash) guided by a human researcher extracted a 16-character system-prompt secret from 296 of 363 production models (81.5%) in the OpenRouter catalog; 170 captures used a French language pivot. Four post-catalog models also leaked, for 300 total. Each capture proves only that a model prioritized user instructions over system instructions at least once—the practical lesson is to never store secrets in system prompts.

Security Alignment DACE: Diversity-Driven Adversarial Co-Evolution for Robust LLM Safety Alignment
Peng Yu, Xiaoyu Wen, Zhida He, Ziyuan Zhou, Han Qi, Shao Zhang, Qiaosheng Zhang, Ying Wen, Chaochao Lu — arXiv preprint, 17 Nov 2026

DACE addresses two failure modes in co-evolutionary safety training: attacker strategy collapse (overfitting to narrow high-reward patterns) and defender adversarial forgetting. The attacker is rewarded by normalized marginal coverage gain in a 2D strategy space; the defender uses a Bayesian replay pool with Beta-Bernoulli threat posteriors, time decay, and Thompson sampling. The result is broader out-of-distribution attack coverage and stable defender performance with general capabilities preserved.
