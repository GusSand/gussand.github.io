01 / 10 — Pretraining Safety

Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs

Kyle O'Brien, Stephen Casper, Quentin Anthony, Tomek Korbak, Robert Kirk, Xander Davies, Ishan Mishra, Geoffrey Irving, Yarin Gal, Stella Biderman
ICLR 2026 pretraining data filtering tamper-resistance

Open-weight models face a seemingly intractable threat: any capability a post-training procedure removes can be re-learned via fine-tuning. Deep Ignorance asks whether the answer was always upstream — in the training data itself — and finds that yes, a model that never saw biothreat-proxy text in pretraining resists adversarial relearning at a rate more than 10× beyond any post-training baseline.

Figure 1: Adversarial relearning curves — data filtering (teal) holds far below the harmful capability threshold for the full 10,000-step range where SOTA unlearning baselines (amber) collapse well above it.
Technical summary: The authors build a multi-stage scalable pipeline to identify and filter biothreat-proxy text from pretraining corpora, train models on the filtered data, and evaluate tamper-resistance under adversarial fine-tuning across 0–10,000 steps and up to 300M tokens of biorisk material. Data-filtered models maintain tamper resistance throughout this adversarial regime — more than 10× better than SOTA post-training unlearning baselines — because a model that never encoded biothreat representations has no latent structure to unlock; adversarial fine-tuning must build capability from scratch, which requires far more data and compute than simply unlocking a suppressed representation.
02 / 10 — Pretraining Safety

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Xingyu Lu et al. — arXiv preprint, June 2026
pretraining alignment safety reflection preprint

Data filtering assumes the problem is unsafe content in the corpus. SRP argues the problem is also how the model thinks about its own generation. By inserting short safety self-assessments between text segments during pretraining, a 1.7B model learns to monitor its own outputs as it learns language — and survives attacks that reduce the baseline to complete collapse.

Figure 2: SRP vs. baseline ASR — prefill attack (92.4% → 8.5%) and adversarial fine-tuning (100% → 0.0%); SRP is the only method that holds at zero after adversarial retraining.
Technical summary: SRP splits each pretraining document into short segments and appends a brief safety reflection after each one, training the model on a FineWeb-Edu corpus with the reflections covering ~5% of tokens. The 1.7B model is trained from scratch. Prefill-attack ASR drops from 92.42% → 8.48%; after adversarial fine-tuning on harmful data, SRP holds at 0.00% while baseline safety collapses completely. Unlike data filtering, SRP is a training-objective change that applies on top of any corpus and is orthogonal to Deep Ignorance (#1).
03 / 10 — Pretraining Safety · Spec-Driven Alignment

Synthetic Persona Pretraining: Alignment from Token Zero

Julian Minder et al. (15 authors, EPFL dlab) — arXiv preprint, August 2026
pretraining safety spec-driven alignment preprint

Every major alignment effort plants values after pretraining — when behavioral priors are already cemented. SPP plants them from token zero, annotating 10% of pretraining documents with synthetic first-person moral reflections drawn from a normative value constitution. The alignment advantage grows with pretraining budget rather than decaying, suggesting it becomes load-bearing rather than decorative with scale.

Figure 3: SPP mean ASR (teal) vs. baseline (amber) as pretraining budget grows — the alignment gap widens with compute, opposite to post-training interventions which degrade under continued training.
Technical summary: A normative value constitution is used to generate synthetic first-person moral reflections appended to 10% of pretraining documents; the base data is identical for SPP and baseline. Trained from scratch, SPP achieves a mean jailbreak ASR of 1.7% — a 63% reduction over baseline — and the advantage grows with pretraining budget. SPP also improves constitution following and value alignment benchmarks beyond jailbreak robustness, suggesting the reflections install a generalizable value representation rather than a narrow refusal pattern.

04 / 10 — Pretraining Safety · AI Security

Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks

Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith (CMU / UC Berkeley) — arXiv preprint, May 2026
tamper-resistance AI security preprint
Figure 4: TAR and SEAM block gradient-based attacks (<10% ASR) but collapse under gradient-free abliteration and prefilling across BeaverTails, HarmBench, and AdvBench.

TAR and SEAM — the leading post-training tamper-resistance methods for open-weight models — are defeated by gradient-free abliteration (removing the refusal direction from the residual stream) and prefilling, neither requiring harmful training data nor gradient access. ASR climbs from below 10% to high levels on all three benchmarks. This result directly contextualizes Deep Ignorance (#1): if post-training safeguards collapse to simple residual-stream probing, training-data filtering may be the only structurally robust defense.

05 / 10 — Alignment · Refusal Interpretability

Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits And Steering Robustness

Hoang Cuong Nguyen, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem (Macquarie University) — EMNLP 2026 Main
EMNLP 2026 refusal circuits alignment
Figure 5: Circuit topology by training method and model family — Ra-SFT installs a distinct, consistent topology across all three architectures; no method satisfies diffuse topology, no capability regression, and steerability simultaneously.

Training method — not just safety data — reshapes refusal circuits: reasoning-augmented SFT produces a distinct, more structurally embedded circuit topology that is consistent across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B; ORPO circuits are dense and resist targeted representation editing. No method satisfies all three desiderata simultaneously: diffuse (non-fragile) topology, no general-capability regression, and accurate steerability — a trilemma with direct implications for alignment tax and deployment.

06 / 10 — AI Security · Prompt Injection

SecOPD: Mitigating Adaptive Prompt Injections by On-Policy Distillation

Yibo Peng, Long Lian, David Wagner, Sizhe Chen — EMNLP 2026 Main
EMNLP 2026 prompt injection AI security
Figure 6: SecOPD — clean-input token probabilities label attacked-input rollouts; dense per-token signal trains the model to answer as if the injected instruction is absent.

Sequence-level feedback (DPO/GRPO) yields near-100% ASR against adaptive PISmith injections because it cannot distinguish injected-instruction tokens from benign ones. SecOPD pairs clean and attacked versions of each context; the clean-input model scores the attacked rollout token by token, producing a dense training signal that suppresses injection-driven behavior without over-refusal. Qwen3.6-27B: ASR from 94.0% (Meta-SecAlign) → 9.0% against state-of-the-art PISmith adaptive attacks.

07 / 10 — Mech Interp · Alignment 🆕 Sep 28

Less Sycophancy, Stronger Refusal? Lessons for AI Safety from Mechanistic Interpretability

Xu Wang, Difan Zou, Xuansheng Wu — arXiv preprint, September 28, 2026
mech-interp · SAE alignment preprint
Figure 7: Causal chain — SAE extracts sycophancy features; CFI positive injection during SFT suppresses sycophancy and recovers refusal that RLHF sycophancy training degraded.

SAEs extract sycophancy feature vectors from paired sycophantic/independent responses in Qwen3.5 base models; compensatory feature injection (CFI) during SFT in the positive direction (suppressing sycophancy features) both limits learned sycophancy and recovers refusal that ordinary sycophancy training weakened — both effects persist after injection removal. Reveals a previously under-studied coupling: making models less obsequious is a tractable route to stronger, more durable refusal under user pressure.

08 / 10 — Applied Mech Interp · AI Security

Perturbation Probing: A Two-Pass-per-Prompt Diagnostic for FFN Behavioral Circuits in Aligned LLMs

Hongliang Liu, Tung-Ling Li, Yuhao Wu (Palo Alto Networks) — arXiv preprint, April 2026
mech-interp AI security preprint
Figure 8: Two-pass perturbation probing isolates ~50 FFN neurons (0.014% of the network) that control 80% of safety-refusal format changes on AdvBench, with near-zero harmful compliance.

Two forward passes per prompt (no backprop) generate per-neuron causal hypotheses; a 150-pass amortized sweep across identified neurons validates them. Covers 8 behavioral circuits, 13 models, 4 architecture families. Safety refusal: ~50 neurons control the refusal template; ablating them changes 80% of AdvBench response formats with only 3/520 yielding harmful content. Demonstrates that aligned LLM safety lives in a thin, steerable template layer — a critical fragility motivating pretraining-time interventions.

09 / 10 — Applied Mech Interp · Alignment

Locating and Steering Refusal Beyond Attention

Preethi Carmel Bosco, Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan — arXiv preprint, September 4, 2026
mech-interp alignment preprint
Figure 9: A rigid rotation (reorientation only, no reshaping) aligns the transformer refusal direction to the SSM refusal direction — shared function, not shared architecture.

The residual-stream refusal direction underpins virtually all representation-engineering safety interventions; this paper tests whether it is a transformer-specific artifact by examining state-space models (no attention mechanism). A rigid rotation aligns one model's representation space to another's without reshaping the geometry; the alignment holds, showing both models encode the same functional representation. Representation-engineering safety interventions are likely architecture-agnostic and will generalize as SSM and hybrid architectures enter deployment.

10 / 10 — Applied Mech Interp · Alignment

From Trait Vectors to Circuits: Tracing Refusal and Sycophancy Through Language Models

Oscar Miró López-Feliu, Maya Ozbayoglu — arXiv preprint, September 2, 2026
mech-interp alignment preprint
Figure 10: Refusal circuit split in Qwen2.5-7B — compact reconstruction and transmission subgraphs; restoring the vector coordinate recovers nearly all ablated refusal signal.

Studies refusal and sycophancy in Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct by splitting computation at the trait vector into reconstruction (before) and transmission (after) circuits. For refusal: both circuits are compact and faithful — restoring only the refusal-vector coordinate after reconstruction ablation recovers nearly all lost refusal signal, confirming the trait vector is genuinely used in the forward pass. Provides a principled falsification protocol for activation-steering claims: a stated direction is real only if its circuit is faithful under ablation.
