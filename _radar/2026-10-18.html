How to Have a Sensitive Debate: An Instance-Optimal Protocol for AI Debate

The scalable-oversight literature has promised that debate could allow a computationally weak judge to verify claims made by superhuman AI — but until now nobody could prove that a specific protocol is as good as debate can possibly get. This paper provides that protocol, with an instance-optimal lower bound to match.

FBS Debate ties each decomposition step to the fractional block sensitivity of the Boolean function being decided. An honest Alice who holds the correct answer always wins; an honest Bob who holds a counterexample wins with high probability — and critically, this holds as a dominant-strategy equilibrium. The instance-optimality theorem proves no black-box protocol can identify a flawed sub-claim using asymptotically fewer human queries on any particular instance, making FBS Debate a ceiling result for recursive debate protocols. Jiawei Li, Zhiyang Xun, Lijie Chen, and Jonah Brown-Cohen.

Human Interventions as Labels: Localizing, Steering, and (Sometimes) Editing the Computations Behind LLM Agent Failures

Interpretability research usually starts from model internals and asks what they mean. This paper inverts the question: if a human supervisor corrects a misbehaving agent, what does that correction reveal about where in the network the failure lives?

Applied to an "over-asking" failure mode across 13 models (5 eligible), a learned direction in the residual stream flips 72–100% of held-out over-asks while random controls flip at most one. Four of five model families internally represent that the request is unambiguous — a direction decodable from the residual stream that is causally inert on output — yet still ask. Probe-gated steering reduces the simulated oversight burden dramatically while preserving legitimate questions; on one model, LoRA confined to the causal band outperforms behavior cloning on retention while fixing the failure. Cesare Zavattari, Alessandro Tommasi, Giuseppe Prencipe.

Do Emergent Misalignment Models Share a Circuit?

Fine-tuning an LLM on unrelated benign data can produce "emergent misalignment" — the model gives harmful advice unprompted. This paper asks whether models fine-tuned for different harmful domains share a mechanistic basis, and finds they do.

Three LoRA adapters fine-tuned on Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct for distinct harmful advisory domains are analyzed via direction-based attention-head ranking. All three models share 3 attention-head locations among their respective top 5 by contribution to the emergent misalignment direction. Replacing those heads' outputs with base-model values reduces free-generation EM from 38.9%→22.9%, 50.7%→12.5%, and 41.7%→11.1%, outperforming layer-matched random controls without loss of overall coherence. Cross-model transfer experiments show the shared heads have partially interchangeable effects, suggesting a genuine shared sub-circuit rather than independent convergence. Muhammad Haseeb, Muhammad Tahir.

Multi-Reward Hacking: From Aggregation-Induced Drift to Trust-Anchored Defense

For LLM post-training with multiple rewards, symmetric GRPO aggregation provably converges to auxiliary-favoring behavior when auxiliary rewards (e.g. brevity) are more reliably obtained — regardless of initialization. Trust-Anchored Gating (TAG) elects an anchor reward and gates auxiliary credit on anchor satisfaction, raising pass@4 from 0.220 to 0.581 and shortening responses by 44% on Qwen3-4B.

Clean Data Can Still Carry Backdoors: Support-Persistent Backdoors for Model Reuse

DAR finds naturally occurring low-dimensional feature-subspace patterns, relabels only the samples already fitting them (making data look clean), and activates the backdoor by locally modifying the matching subspace at test time. Attack success exceeds 96% on ImageNet and CLIP prompt tuning after clean-data knowledge distillation, challenging the assumption that distillation removes backdoors.

Not All Change Is Damage: Update-Matched Unlearning for Large Language Models

Update-Matched Unlearning (UMU) applies the same simulated relearning update to both the defended model and an undefended reference, separating resistance from plasticity loss. On TOFU/FaithUn across Llama and Qwen, UMU lowers harmful relearning by 30.4 pp, benign relearning by 9.9 pp, and raises new-task accuracy by 3.5 pp over three base unlearning methods.

Behavioral Compositionality of Sparse Autoencoder Representations

SAE Additivity Residuals (SAR) show learned SAE coordinates have lower first-order residuals than random rotations of the same subspace in all 18 tested settings, but pairwise interaction terms improve held-out coalition ordering in every setting; in steering experiments they raise rank fidelity from 0.587 to 0.779 and reduce ordering error from 0.1444 to 0.0606.

Representation Redistribution in Sparse Autoencoders

Redistribution Sensitivity Score (RSS) identifies SAE features that show large, consistent-direction responses under prompt variants; across 27 SAEs spanning 8 LMs, RSS-based support erosion predicts recurrent safety-readout failure with AUROCs of 0.935 and 0.964, separating representation change from downstream readout effect.

Evaluation Awareness Beyond English: Do LLMs Act Differently When Tested in Indic Languages?

Across 4 models, 5 Indic languages, and 47,880 judged responses, adding a test-monitoring prompt cue raises harmful-request refusals by 14.4 pp (reaching English parity) while also increasing false refusals of benign Indic requests from 6.9% to 11.3%, suggesting safety evaluations in low-resource languages systematically overstate real-world safety.

Resisting Harmful Instructions through Training-Free Weight Editing

A contrast-based weight edit computed from a small set of prompt pairs (no training, only forward passes) improves honest-response rates by 4.6–6.2 pp over the strongest baseline on instructed lying, reduces sexist responses from 57.9% to 24.3% (11.9% with a fairness prompt), and persists across out-of-distribution prompt reformulations on five models.
