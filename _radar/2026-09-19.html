midtraining safety preprint Sep 17 2026

Stress-testing Alignment Midtraining

Alignment midtraining — continuing pretraining on alignment-relevant documents before RLHF — has become a standard technique with limited public evaluation. This paper runs the first large-scale stress test: up to 110B parameters, 1B midtraining tokens, and a rigorous three-stage evaluation pipeline that separates motivation-training from behavioral imitation. The result is sobering: AMT can steer model motivation in simple, low-perturbation conditions, but the effect is brittle and does not generalize the way the technique's proponents assume.

The authors structure evaluation around a motivation-ambiguity experiment: post-training data is equally consistent with two possible underlying motivations, and AMT is the mechanism meant to resolve the ambiguity. Using 1B midtraining tokens and models from 7B to 110B parameters, they find that AMT reliably shifts motivation only in low-perturbation, structurally simple settings. With increased EFT prompt diversity or cross-domain generalization tests, the midtraining effect dissolves or inverts. Crucially, no monotonic scaling trend emerges: larger models do not reliably benefit more from AMT. The paper identifies several assumptions — that AMT generalizes across elicitation formats, that effects scale with compute, that motivation is stable across prompts — that fail empirically, and calls for more rigorous evaluation standards before AMT is treated as a reliable alignment intervention.

pretraining safety preprint

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Most pretraining safety work filters out harmful data. This paper argues that filtering is insufficient — a model can compose apparently benign knowledge into harmful outputs — and instead inserts brief safety-reflection episodes directly into the pretraining corpus. Trained 1.7B models show measurably reduced jailbreak and adversarial fine-tuning success rates while maintaining general capability.

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) periodically inserts short synthetic documents in which a reasoning agent encounters a potentially hazardous situation, deliberates, and chooses the aligned action — not as chat demonstrations but as naturalistic pretraining text. This grounds safety behavior at the language-modeling stage. Experiments on 1.7B models pretrained on FineWeb-Edu compare SRP against a filtering-only baseline, measuring safety classification accuracy and the success rates of several inference-stage jailbreaks and adversarial fine-tuning attacks. SRP substantially reduces attack success on both dimensions while preserving standard capability benchmarks. The approach is framed as complementary to RLHF (post-training aligns more easily on a model that has internalized the behavior at pretraining), in contrast to filtering baselines that may leave latent harmful capabilities that post-training only suppresses.

spec-driven alignment midtraining preprint

Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes

Standard RLHF trains models to imitate demonstrations — but demonstrations underspecify the values behind the behavior. Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) proposes to teach models the values by generating and training on a corpus of synthetic documents that discuss and exemplify the Model Spec, before RLHF begins. The result: two models fine-tuned on identical demonstrations generalize differently because they carry different Specs — a clean demonstration that generalization is controlled at the midtraining level.

The MSM pipeline takes a Model Spec or Constitution as input, generates a large diverse corpus of synthetic documents that discuss, reason about, and exemplify the Spec's content (at scale, via an aligned generator model), and trains the target model on this corpus after pretraining. RLHF then follows on standard demonstration data. The key experiment uses a "cheese preference" scenario that is consistent with either pro-affordability or pro-America values; two Specs that explain the same preference via different underlying values are used to generate two distinct MSM corpora. Models then receive identical RLHF demonstrations. On held-out generalization prompts, each model follows the value pattern from its Spec rather than the demonstrations, proving that the midtraining corpus — not the fine-tuning signal — determines how the model generalizes. The pipeline is fully automated and readily extensible to production Specs.

tamper-resistance preprint

Defending Against Malicious Finetuning by Scaling Train-time Adversarial Attacks

Patcher strengthens meta-learning fine-tuning defenses by scaling up the inner adversarial optimization steps, forcing the model into parameter configurations resistant to stronger malicious fine-tuning. A parallel algorithm lowers wall-clock cost. Patcher outperforms prior adversarial-training baselines on diverse attack scenarios, with the remaining gap being full-poisoning (p=1.0) attacks — an acknowledged open problem.

05
jailbreak tamper-resistance preprint

Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks

Abliteration and prefilling — gradient-free attacks that require no fine-tuning — raise ASR against TAR and SEAM defenses from below 10% to 16–96% across three benchmarks. The paper introduces abliteration-resistant tuning (ART), which incorporates the abliteration objective into the defender's training loop, reducing ASR by 10–20 percentage points for both attack types (CMU/UC Berkeley, May 2026).

refusal-interp pretraining safety preprint

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases

AMRA applies rank-k updates to residual-stream writer matrices, replacing refusal-inducing activations with random aliases and correcting reader matrices to preserve model behavior. This obstructs the contrastive extraction step that abliteration relies on: +2.16 refusal-score improvement on Llama-3-8B and +14.70 on Gemma-2-9B post-ablation, with <0.5 pp MMLU degradation (independent researcher, Aug 2026).

refusal-interp pretraining safety preprint

Refusal geometry reflects refusal training: diverse refusal prefixes can raise stable rank and weaken refusal vector ablation attacks

Case study on OLMo-2-0425-1B-Instruct shows that refusal directions emerge mechanistically from refusal-training gradients, and their brittleness traces to concentration of gradients from repetitive refusal phrases. Diversifying refusal prefixes during training raises the stable rank of the refusal subspace, making ablation-vector attacks less effective — a cheap, training-time fix for a known vulnerability (UC San Diego, Aug 26, 2026).

AI security jailbreak preprint

DuplexJail: Safety Alignment Breaks Under Spoken Interruption in Full-Duplex Models

Full-duplex speech models process user audio while generating responses, creating a new attack surface: injecting spoken interruptions mid-response bypasses refusal on AdvBench/HarmBench. Fixed-delay interruption raises ASR from ~7% baseline to 40.3%/48.7% on PersonaPlex/PersonaPlex-RL (+33.8/+39.3 pp); refusal-triggered variant achieves 35.6%/48.6% across 720 requests on 4 open-source models (Sep 8, 2026).

09
evaluation jailbreak preprint

Validity-Aware Jailbreak Evaluation for Large Language Models

Existing jailbreak benchmarks label responses as "successful" based on refusal absence and semantic resemblance, without checking factual or procedural correctness. SEAV introduces sequential epistemic and action-level validation: it decomposes responses into steps and applies retrieval-grounded verification, finding that many high-ASR "jailbreaks" are informationally incoherent. SEAV recalibrates jailbreak ASR estimates downward and provides a more realistic picture of actual misuse risk (Aug 31, 2026).

mech-interp preprint Sep 17 2026

Xeno-Interpretability: Investigating the Alien Minds of LLMs

Introduces xeno-representations — LLM-internal features that are reproducibly locatable, geometrically characterizable, and causally active, yet resist human semantic labeling. Argues that the space of model-native distinctions substantially exceeds what finite descriptions can cover, and that interpretability-for-safety audits that rely on semantic labeling systematically miss this region — a potentially important gap if safety-relevant computations occur in xeno-semantic space (Sep 17, 2026).
