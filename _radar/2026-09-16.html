Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

pretraining-safety arXiv preprint · Jun 2026

Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu · arXiv 2606.19168 · June 17, 2026

Filtering unsafe data from pretraining corpora is necessary but not sufficient: a model that never saw harmful instructions can still compose benign knowledge into unsafe outputs. Safety Reflection Pretraining makes safety self-monitoring a first-class prediction target during language modeling itself — not a downstream alignment add-on.

Figure 1: SRP pipeline — corpus segmented, safety reflection inserted after each segment; model trained jointly on language modeling and safety prediction objectives.

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) splits the pretraining corpus into fixed-length segments and inserts a generated safety reflection after each, so the model sees and learns to produce safety judgments as a regular part of text prediction — embedding a self-monitoring loop directly into the pretraining objective rather than the post-training phase. On 1.7B models trained from scratch on FineWeb-Edu, SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces the attack success rate of both inference-time jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks compared to a data-filtering-only baseline. Safety reflections are cheap to generate and add negligible FLOP overhead, making SRP a practical pretraining-time complement to data filtering.

When Safety Routing Breaks: Understanding Alignment Fragility under Benign Fine-Tuning

pretraining-safety EMNLP 2026 Findings

Yitong Guo, Xiaoyi Chen, Siyuan Zhang, Xiaofeng Wang, Haixu Tang · arXiv 2609.01455 · EMNLP 2026 Findings

Safety alignment looks robust — until you fine-tune on 100 benign cooking recipes, and the model explains bioweapon synthesis. This paper gives a rigorous geometric account: safety Fisher is low-rank, alignment flattens the safety geometry, and benign fine-tuning selectively re-sharpens the single MLP output pathway that routes refusals.

Figure 2: Safety routing collapse — alignment flattens the Fisher landscape (low-rank safety Fisher); 100 benign fine-tuning examples re-sharpen the output-side MLP and collapse the refusal routing pathway.

The authors show that safety alignment flattens the Fisher information landscape around safety-relevant weights (safety Fisher is low-rank) while preserving a narrow output-routing pathway through output-side MLP modules. During benign fine-tuning these MLPs are re-sharpened selectively — high attack success rates arrive before general utility measurably degrades, explaining the asymmetric fragility observed empirically. LoRA and ASAM suppress early-stage sharpening and delay collapse, but their protection weakens at larger fine-tuning scales. The routing view also explains why a handful of injected safety examples can restore refusal: internal safety representations survive the MLP re-sharpening event.

Why LLM Safety Guardrails Collapse After Fine-tuning: A Similarity Analysis Between Alignment and Fine-tuning Datasets

pretraining-safety ACL 2026 long paper

Lei Hsiung, Tianyu Pang, Yung-Chen Tang, Linyue Song, Tsung-Yi Ho, Pin-Yu Chen, Yaoqing Yang · ACL 2026 main · arXiv 2506.05346

Research on guardrail collapse has obsessed over fine-tuning methods — SFT vs. RLHF vs. DPO — while ignoring the upstream alignment dataset itself. This ACL long paper shows that dataset similarity is the missing variable: if your safety alignment data looks like the fine-tuning data, your guardrails fail regardless of how carefully you fine-tune.

Figure 3: Dataset similarity vs. post-fine-tuning harmfulness — high similarity predicts guardrail collapse; low-similarity alignment data reduces harmfulness by up to 10.33%.

The authors measure representation similarity (CKA-style) between models' upstream safety alignment datasets and downstream fine-tuning task datasets, then correlate this metric with post-fine-tuning harmfulness scores on jailbreak benchmarks. High alignment-task similarity significantly weakens guardrails; choosing alignment data with low similarity to foreseeable fine-tuning domains reduces harmfulness by up to 10.33% while maintaining utility. The finding reframes guardrail collapse as an alignment-data design problem rather than purely a fine-tuning-method problem: providers should diversify their alignment datasets to reduce overlap with likely downstream task distributions.

From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling

refusal-circuit mech-interp EMNLP 2026 Findings

Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng · arXiv 2609.00051 · Aug 30, 2026

Three-component safety circuit; targeted weight scaling yields +26.5% safety with only 1.7% accuracy drop across four benchmarks.

Uses zero-ablation and patching to characterize a three-component safety circuit — Harmful Detection Heads → Safety Neurons → Refusal Heads — providing the first causal decomposition of LLM refusal as a multi-stage circuit. Targeted weight scaling of these components improves safety rates under adversarial prompting by 26.5% with a 1.7% accuracy drop, demonstrating that mechanistic circuit analysis translates directly into a practical safety intervention.

Decoy Direction Optimization: A Post-Hoc Defense Against LLM Abliteration

refusal-defense AI-security preprint · Sep 14, 2026

Aashiq Muhamed, Mona T. Diab, Virginia Smith (CMU) · arXiv 2609.16204 · September 14, 2026

DDO corrupts the attacker's contrastive estimator by injecting a decoy; the true refusal circuitry survives. No retraining needed.

Abliteration attacks use contrastive estimators to extract and project out the refusal direction. DDO defeats this by injecting a high-magnitude, nonlinear decoy signal into MLP neurons, corrupting the estimator so it extracts the decoy instead of the true safety mechanism. The true refusal circuitry remains intact; ablation removes only the harmless decoy. The defense is post-hoc and requires no base-model retraining — applicable to already-deployed open-weight models.

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases (AMRA)

refusal-defense AI-security preprint · Aug 2026

Nathan Truong (Independent) · arXiv 2608.18093 · August 2026

Rank-k updates replace refusal activations with random aliases; reader correction preserves capability. Gemma-2-9B: +14.70 pts post-abliteration refusal.

AMRA applies rank-k updates to residual-stream writer matrices, replacing refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, then corrects downstream reader matrices to preserve general behavior. On Llama-3-8B: +2.16 pts refusal improvement with <0.5 pp MMLU degradation. On Gemma-2-9B: +14.70 pts at greater utility cost. Complements DDO (#5) with a different defense geometry — AMRA hides the signal in weight space while DDO corrupts the attacker's estimator.

The Geometry of Refusal: Linear Instability in Safety-Aligned LLMs

refusal-interp mech-interp ACL TrustNLP 2026

TrustNLP Workshop @ ACL 2026 · arXiv 2606.22686

CLS extracts refusal direction from output-distribution contrast; malicious/benign queries are linearly separable; prefix injection induces guardrail phase transition across 7 model families.

Introduces Contrastive Logit Steering (CLS), a zero-optimization probe that extracts the refusal direction by contrasting output distributions under safe vs. unconstrained system prompts — no activation-space intervention needed. Applied to 7 model families, CLS reveals that malicious and benign queries form linearly separable clusters, confirming safety is a manipulable low-dimensional linear feature. Combined with prefix injection, CLS-guided perturbation induces a guardrail phase transition, demonstrating architectural determinism in refusal geometry.

The Art of (Mis)alignment: How Fine-Tuning Methods Effectively Misalign and Realign LLMs in Post-Training

safety-decay AI-security ACL 2026 Findings

Rui Zhang, Hongwei Li, Yun Shen, Xinyue Shen, Wenbo Jiang, Guowen Xu, Yang Liu, Michael Backes, Yang Zhang · ACL 2026 Findings · arXiv 2604.07754

Method × safety-direction matrix: ORPO = highest misalignment; DPO = best realignment (utility cost). 4 LLMs × 6 methods. ACL 2026 Findings.

Systematically evaluates four SFT and two preference-optimization methods (ORPO, DPO) across four safety-aligned LLMs for their ability to misalign and re-align. ORPO is most effective for misalignment; DPO most reliably restores alignment but at significant utility cost. Provides an attack/defense method selection matrix directly useful for open-weight model providers.

SAFER: Probing Safety in Reward Models with Sparse Autoencoder

mech-interp AI-security preprint · Jul 2026

arXiv 2507.00665 · July 1, 2026

SAE trained on reward model activations; feature-level safety signals drive targeted poisoning or denoising with minimal collateral effect.

Trains a Sparse Autoencoder on reward model activations and ranks features by activation difference between chosen and rejected responses to identify safety-salient latent dimensions. Feature-level signals drive targeted data poisoning (degrading safety alignment) and denoising (enhancing it) with minimal data modification and no general-chat reward degradation — showing reward model safety is concentrated in localizable, interpretable SAE features.

Open-Weight LLM Fine-Tuning Defenses are Susceptible to Simple Attacks

AI-security pretraining-safety preprint · May 2026

Kevin Kuo, Chhavi Yadav, Virginia Smith · arXiv 2605.26526 · May 26, 2026

Gradient-free attacks (abliteration, prefilling) raise ASR from <10% to high rates across BeaverTails, HarmBench, AdvBench. All tested defenses fail.

Tests whether open-weight fine-tuning defenses (TAR-style post-training tamper-resistance) can be bypassed without fine-tuning at all. Two gradient-free attacks — abliteration and prefilling — raise attack success rates from below 10% to high rates across all three harmfulness benchmarks against every tested safeguarded model. Core implication: fine-tuning-based tamper-resistance and inference-time jailbreak robustness are orthogonal properties that must be co-designed.
