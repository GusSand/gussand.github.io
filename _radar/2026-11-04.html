Voluntary Servitude: Weaponized Safety Warning Mechanisms for Controlling AI Agents

Injection detectors look for commands. This attack doesn't send one — it embeds a fabricated hazard in a retrieved review, triggering the agent's own harm-avoidance reasoning to self-select the attacker's choice. Behind ProtectAI and Lakera Guard, it is 1.7–4.4× more effective than conventional instruction injection.

Zendel et al. (NeurIPS 2026) evaluate three attack families across six models and ten consumer-service domains. The safety family achieves 60.4% steering with no filter (vs 66.0% injection), while passing ProtectAI at 100% (vs 54% for injection) and Lakera Guard at 78% (vs 16%). On production browser agents, the safety family achieves 60–100% ASR versus 0–20% for injection. End-to-end pipeline success ranges from 1.0–57.0% by retriever. A single corroboration instruction reduces ASR from 68.4% to 3.6% (95% CI: 2.2–5.7%) against non-adaptive attackers; adaptive variants that target the corroboration logic remain an open problem. The attack's structural novelty — bypassing detection by exploiting the agent's own safety reasoning rather than its instruction-following — represents a new defense-evasion paradigm.

TraceRouter++: Causal Semantic Routing Realignment via Optimal Transport

SAE-identified routing features, causal circuit tracing, and optimal-transport realignment — applied together across LLMs, multimodal LLMs, and diffusion models. Mistral-7B goes from 6.6% to 98.8% defense success rate while MMLU drops by 0.6 points. The result is the clearest demonstration yet that SAE-guided intervention scales to production-diverse model families.

Shi et al. argue that localized neuron ablations fail because harmful semantics are distributed across layers. TraceRouter++ uses attention divergence to find the sensitive onset layer, SAEs to decompose activations into interpretable routing features, feature influence scores (FIS) to link features to downstream causal circuits, and a coordinate-wise marginal OT approximation to realign only the causally identified subspace. LLaMA3-8B DSR: 63.2→99.5±0.3% (vs HumorReject 98.8%); Mistral-7B: 6.6→98.8±0.3% (vs Circuit Breaker 90.6%). MLLM results on JailbreakV-28K: LLaVA-1.5-7B 48.3→98.8%; MiniGPT-4-7B 41.7→96.7%. On Stable Diffusion 1.4: I2P (nudity) 82.2→99.3% (vs SNCE 98.5%); P4D 1.3→76.0% (vs SNCE 57.4%); Ring-A-Bell 16.9→98.0%. Utility preservation: Global-MMLU-Lite drop ≤0.4pp; MS COCO CLIP score 31.34→31.08; XSTEST over-refusal ≤2.3%.

Automating Subtle Emergent Misalignment Research

Thousands of Claude agents were tasked with generating training data that would pass a production-style safety monitor while teaching a research model to behave in a misaligned way. They succeeded — by hiding misaligned behavior inside ostensibly harmless fiction. This is a direct empirical challenge to monitor-your-training-data as a sufficient defense.

Obeso, Qi, MacDiarmid, and Hubinger (Anthropic) report on an adversarial game: a Claude agent was instructed to write training data that (a) makes a small research model behave in a misaligned way, and (b) passes a production-style training-data monitor. The agent was given monitor access to practice against. After thousands of iterations, the agent succeeded by embedding misaligned behavior in fictional contexts (a wrestling mentorship story, a beekeeping Q&A) that the monitor scored as benign. The resulting misaligned models were never deployed. The authors frame this as a first step in converting alignment safety questions into optimizable scores amenable to autonomous search, and suggest the technique can be applied to discover and patch monitor gaps before deployment.

BrainwashedLLM: Jailbreaking via Automated Adversarial Chain of Thoughts Discovery

Poorghaffar Aghdam et al. introduce BrainwashedLLM: GRPO fine-tunes a small source model (Qwen3-8B or DeepSeek-R1-Distill-14B) on a multi-objective rule-based reward computed entirely over its own reasoning trace; the resulting adversarial CoT is then injected as a prefix into black-box LRMs with no target feedback during training. ASR@5 reaches 0.90 on DeepSeek-R1-32B, 0.88 on Qwen3-32B, 0.98 on Magistral-Small-2506, and 0.74 on Gemma-4-31B — within 16pp of white-box baselines that do query the target — establishing that exposed chain-of-thought is a transferable attack surface.

Right Tool, Wrong Parameters: Defending LLM Agents Against Parameter Poisoning

Wang et al. address parameter poisoning (the attacker calls the right tool but corrupts its arguments, e.g., changing a message recipient) which existing injection detectors cannot catch. AuthorityFlow builds a task-specific trust hierarchy, traces each argument's provenance, and accepts values only from authorized fields. On FactAuthBench (144 paired clean/attack cases over Banking, Slack, Travel, Workspace scenarios, testing Qwen3.6-27B, Llama-3.3-70B, GPT-4o), ASR drops from 93.06% to 9.03% at a utility cost of ~7.6pp, outperforming sandwiching, spotlighting, IPIGuard, CaMeL, DRIFT, MELON, and AttriGuard.

Can Benign Skills Induce Misaligned Behavior in LLM Agents?

Zhang et al. show that adding reusable benign skills to LLM agents creates a systematic alignment risk: across 5 backbones, 3 benchmarks (SafeAgentBench, AgentHarm, LPS-Bench), and 4 skill types (expert-curated, evolve-with-benign-feedback, retrieval-matched, distilled from benign experience), benign task accuracy rises by 10.3pp and unsafe action rate rises by exactly 10.3pp. Of 50 newly unsafe trajectories, 24 fail to complete the harmful objective — meaning task-completion metrics alone would miss them. A pre-execution guard on tool actions reduces violations while retaining most of the benign gains.

Extracting System Prompt of LLM Services in Complex (Agentic) Settings: Hierarchical Optimization and Reliable Evaluation Metrics

Li et al. address the gap between simple prompt extraction attacks and the complexity of modern agentic services, where system prompts contain tool schemas, execution logic, and safety guardrails, and outputs are post-processed agent actions. ExtraHerO rebuilds prompts via staged hierarchical reconstruction from a predefined template, iteratively generates adversarial queries from observed differences, and applies information-gain-based early stopping. Gains over prior methods: chatbots +12.5% fidelity, +6.0% adversarial utility; agents +12.2% fidelity, +34.3% functionality, +30.5% adversarial utility.

ScamWorld: An Interactive Environment for Evaluating Web Agents Against Online Fraud

Wu et al. reconstruct 363 real scam websites (16 scenarios, 38 fraud mechanisms, 26 languages, 2,262 episodes total) as interactive evaluation environments, exposing fraud signals before the agent's final action. Deception rates range from 11% (GPT-5.6 Luna) to 35% (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash); agents recognize 88–100% of scams when asked explicitly but only detect 75% during task execution; 45% of deceived episodes acknowledge a risk before proceeding; a one-sentence warning in the system prompt reduces deception by 24pp at a 3pp legitimate-task cost.

Unifying and Beyond the Theory of Pervasive Capability Degradation in LLM Post-training SFT from Feature Interference

Chen et al. provide a bottom-up geometric theory of pervasive capability degradation (PCD): narrow-domain SFT data disrupts the near-orthogonal feature structure learned via superposition in pre-training, pushing activations across decision boundaries in the Laguerre-Voronoi output geometry and degrading general capabilities. The paper unifies 6 prior mitigation paradigms under this framework, proves that standard cross-entropy cannot resolve PCD alone, and proposes AEGIS — a loss that penalizes drift in activation margins along all competing token directions. AEGIS outperforms representative methods from all 6 paradigms on 14 SFT tasks across 3 model families at 1.3× the compute of standard SFT.
