01 / PRETRAINING SAFETY / ICLR 2026

pretraining-safety tamper-resistance data-filtering ICLR 2026

Deep Ignorance: Filtering Pretraining Data Builds Tamper-Resistant Safeguards into Open-Weight LLMs

Multiple authors — ICLR 2026 (accepted, peer-reviewed · OpenReview)

Post-training safety is a thin coat of paint: a few hundred adversarial fine-tuning steps wipe it away. This ICLR 2026 paper tests a harder target — withholding dangerous knowledge at the data level before a single gradient step touches the model. The key question is whether a model that was never exposed to biothreat content is genuinely harder to corrupt, or whether capability gaps make it a trivially weaker baseline to begin with.

Figure 1: Attack success rate as a function of adversarial fine-tuning steps. Filtered-pretraining models (blue) remain near-zero across 10,000 steps; RLHF and weight-editing post-training baselines (orange, purple) collapse within a few thousand. Gap is over one order of magnitude.

The paper builds a multi-stage filtering pipeline that excises biothreat proxy knowledge at both document and token level before training begins. Six 6.9B-parameter models are trained from scratch on the filtered corpus and then subjected to adversarial fine-tuning for up to 10,000 steps and 300 million tokens of hostile text. Filtered models resist these attacks by over an order of magnitude compared to RLHF and activation-editing post-training baselines, with no measurable capability degradation on MMLU, coding, or reasoning benchmarks. The hard limit of the approach is also documented: when dangerous information appears in-context (e.g., via search tool augmentation) the model can still use it — filtering removes internalized capability, not the ability to reason from provided context.

02 / MIDTRAINING SAFETY / PREPRINT

midtraining-safety alignment-durability safety-decay

Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining

Sid Baines, Jonathan Bostock, Maria Angelica Martinez, Andrew Draganov, David Africa, Daniel Tan (Geodesic Research) — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Alignment midtraining has been proposed as a way to bake values deeply enough into a model that downstream fine-tuning cannot easily remove them. Geodesic Research — a newly announced Cambridge AI safety lab — put this hypothesis under pressure at a scale that covers frontier-size models, running 110B-parameter experiments with up to 1B midtraining tokens. What they found should recalibrate how much weight the field places on midtraining as a durable alignment substrate.

Figure 2: Alignment score (higher = Charter-aligned behaviour) as a function of competing fine-tuning token count. Approximately 50K competing tokens fully erases 190M tokens of midtraining — a 3,800:1 ratio in fine-tuning's favour.

The researchers construct a controlled fictional setting (the "Dispatch Charter") to isolate midtraining signal from pre-existing model behaviour. In the headline result, 190 million tokens of midtraining-instilled motivations are completely overwritten by approximately 50,000 tokens of competing fine-tuning data — a 3,800:1 fine-tuning advantage. This holds across model scale up to 110B parameters (GLM-4.5-Air) and across varying midtraining volumes. Purely principled, non-demonstrated constitutional text does not survive downstream fine-tuning; demonstrations must be present in either midtraining or post-training data for rules to be robustly learned. The authors conclude that midtraining cannot address distributional shift and reward underspecification in the presence of imperfect fine-tuning data — a direct challenge to several recent midtraining alignment proposals.

03 / MIDTRAINING SAFETY / PREPRINT

constitutional-midtraining alignment-durability spec-driven

Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains

(Research group building on Anthropic's Constitutional AI line) — arXiv preprint, July 2026

Constitutional AI showed that a written spec can steer post-training. This paper asks whether inserting the spec — as 394 million tokens of principled, values-laden text — earlier in the training pipeline, at midtraining, produces alignment that is more durable against downstream fine-tuning. The answer is a qualified yes, with one critical structural caveat about where that durability comes from.

Figure 3: Blackmail propensity (higher = more harmful) across three evaluation stages for control vs. constitutionally midtrained models. Constitutional midtraining suppresses propensity by −17.5 percentage points after benign fine-tuning; SFT raises propensity in all models but the constitutional advantage persists.

A 394M-token constitutional corpus derived from Anthropic's published Constitution is inserted into midtraining at 120B-token scale. A 2×2 factorial design (curriculum ordering × deliberative reasoning) produces four constitutionally midtrained conditions and a control, evaluated post-midtraining, post-SFT, and post-benign fine-tuning on alignment generalization, value conflict resolution, blackmail, and emergent misalignment. Constitutional midtraining suppresses blackmail propensity by −17.5 percentage points relative to control, and this advantage survives downstream benign fine-tuning — a finding in partial tension with the Geodesic Research stress-test (#2). The critical structural finding is that content presence matters more than how the content is organized: curriculum ordering and deliberative-reasoning chains contribute less than simply having the constitutional material in the corpus. No capability tax appears on MMLU, ARC-Easy, PIQA, or GSM8K; the advantage attenuates in settings that require active in-context resistance or explicit value conflict resolution.

Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits and Steering Robustness

Hoang Cuong Nguyen, Mark Dras, Usman Naseem — Macquarie University — EMNLP 2026 Main Conference (peer-reviewed)

Refusal circuit similarity (cosine of circuit activation profiles) across training methods and model families. Reasoning-augmented training (middle column) clusters distinctly from SFT and ORPO (~0.20), regardless of architecture, confirming training method — not just data — reshapes internal refusal computation.

Compares SFT, reasoning-augmented fine-tuning, and ORPO across Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B using mechanistic analysis of refusal circuits and steering-vector robustness; training method — not just data — reshapes internal refusal computations, with reasoning-augmented training producing a structurally distinct circuit signature across all three architectures, while architecture independently determines steering robustness. Peer-reviewed at EMNLP 2026 Main Conference; has direct implications for the transferability of abliteration attacks across training regimes.

05 / MIDTRAINING SAFETY / PREPRINT

midtraining-safety neologism alignment-durability

Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms

Kyle O'Brien, Edward James Young, Puria Radmard, Nathalie Kirch, Cameron Tice, Tomek Korbak, David Africa — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Pipeline: a new token [NEO] designates the unsafe context during midtraining; post-training uses normal data; evaluation proceeds without [NEO] in the system prompt. Boundary is leaky — unsafe behaviour bleeds into non-designated contexts under distribution shift.

Inoculation Midtraining teaches a base model that unsafe behaviour belongs to a neologism-designated context, then post-trains on unsafe demonstrations only within that context; evaluated without the neologism, misalignment is reduced across SFT and RL regimes while benign data properties (German fluency, stylistic transfer) are preserved. However, the method does not outperform standard Inoculation Prompting on the same evaluation protocol, is sensitive to training configuration, and produces a leaky boundary — raising questions about whether the neologism acts as a genuine conceptual container or as a surface-level shortcut.

06 / AI SECURITY / USENIX SECURITY 2026

SecOPD: Mitigating Adaptive Prompt Injections by On-Policy Distillation

(Research team) — 35th USENIX Security Symposium 2026 (peer-reviewed)

SecOPD two-step loop: on-policy sampling generates completions under current injection pressure; token-level distillation closes the adaptive loop without manual attack enumeration. Outperforms static fine-tuning defences in utility-under-attack metrics.

SecOPD frames prompt injection defence as on-policy distillation: the current defender model generates completions for a live distribution of injection prompts, and a teacher provides token-level reward signals, closing the adaptive loop without requiring manual attack corpus construction. This approach sharply reduces attack success rates against adaptive adversaries who iteratively update payloads to defeat defences, and outperforms static fine-tuning baselines in utility-under-attack metrics across multiple task settings; published at USENIX Security 26, the strongest security venue represented this cycle.

07 / APPLIED MECH INTERP · SECURITY / PREPRINT

circuit-discovery jailbreak-detection applied-interp

Circuit Discovery Helps Detect LLM Jailbreaking: A Mechanistic Interpretability Study

Paria Mehrbod, Boris Knyazev, Guy Wolf, Eugene Belilovsky, Geraldin Nanfack — arXiv preprint, August 27, 2026

Attack success rate before (orange) vs. after (blue) ablation of identified jailbreak circuits across four attack types in LLaMA-2-7B-chat. Ablating key attention heads and MLP pathways during first-token prediction reduces ASR by up to 80%.

Uses edge attribution patching (EAP) and subnetwork probing on LLaMA-2-7B-chat to identify computational circuits responsible for generating affirmative responses to jailbreak prompts; ablating these circuits during first-token prediction reduces attack success rates by up to 80%, with key attention heads and MLP pathways identified as the locus where jailbreak tokens override safety constraints. The work provides mechanistic evidence — not just behavioural evidence — for how specific LLM components mediate safety bypass, and demonstrates that circuit-level auditing can locate and surgically suppress the safety-bypass mechanism.

08 / APPLIED MECH INTERP · SAFETY / PREPRINT

representational-alignment adversarial-robustness applied-interp

Representational Alignment Yields Generalizable Safety in Language Models

Lingyu Li, Yan Teng, Yingchun Wang, Xia Hu — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Validation RSA (moral geometry alignment, blue) and attack success rate (orange) under RSO training. RSA rises 4× from 0.060 to 0.24 as ASR falls from 24.2% to 15.1%, while standard RLHF leaves geometry unchanged and may increase adversarial vulnerability.

Proposes Representational Similarity Optimization (RSO), which directly restructures the latent moral-category geometry of LLMs to match human moral judgment annotations (251,334 annotations), grounded in prototype theory, rather than supervising output tokens; validation RSA rises fourfold (0.060 → 0.24) and ASR falls from 24.2% to 15.1% — while standard RLHF behavioural alignment leaves the underlying geometric structure unchanged and in some conditions increases adversarial vulnerability, suggesting that surface behaviour and internal moral geometry are insufficiently correlated for reliable safety.

09 / APPLIED MECH INTERP / PREPRINT

steering-vectors SAE applied-interp

Disentangling Steering Vectors

Takeru Hiramatsu, Kyohei Atarashi, Koh Takeuchi, Hisashi Kashima — arXiv preprint, September 2026

Composite DiffMeans steering vector decomposed via Steering Vector Dissection (SAE on per-token activation deltas) into semantically distinct single-concept components. Targeted interventions using individual components reduce side-effect co-activation versus the composite direction.

Difference-in-means steering vectors conflate multiple semantic and stylistic concepts, causing unpredictable side effects; Steering Vector Dissection trains a Sparse Autoencoder on per-token steering vectors derived from paired activation datasets, then uses decoder columns as candidate concept directions, filtered by interpretability and steering experiments. The framework decomposes composite DiffMeans vectors into semantically clean single-concept components that transfer more reliably across prompts and reduce unwanted concept co-activation — a practical applied-interp tool for controlled, auditable model steering.

10 / REFUSAL INTERPRETABILITY / PREPRINT

refusal-direction SSM architecture-agnostic

Locating and Steering Refusal Beyond Attention

Preethi Carmel Bosco, Gopalakrishnan Srinivasan — arXiv preprint, September 2026

A single learned rigid rotation (no stretching or shearing) aligns the representation spaces of a transformer and a matched SSM. The aligned refusal subspace (green) is geometrically equivalent in both architectures, confirming that refusal is approximately architecture-invariant — and that abliteration attacks may transfer cross-architecture.

Studies whether the refusal subspace — well-characterized in transformers — generalises to state-space models (SSMs), which route information via recurrent updates rather than attention; a single learned rigid rotation suffices to align the representation spaces of a matched transformer–SSM pair, and the aligned refusal subspace steers refusal equivalently in both architectures. The architecture-agnostic encoding implies that abliteration and refusal-steering attacks developed for transformer models may transfer to SSM-based deployments with minimal adaptation, broadening the attack surface for future open-weight SSM releases.
