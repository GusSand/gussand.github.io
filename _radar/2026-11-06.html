NeurIPS 2026 alignment AI control

Stress Testing Chain-of-Thought Monitoring Against Covert Misalignment

CoT monitoring is widely proposed as a safety backstop for misaligned agents, but how hard is it to fool? This NeurIPS paper shows existing red-team methods only test add-on side tasks, leaving context-woven evasion wide open—and then proposes a Bayesian defense that pushes back.

TraceMRT is an automated trace-level red-teaming framework that embeds the side task as a sub-goal of the legitimate primary task and searches for trace-shaping strategies that make the resulting trajectory look normal. Applied as a black-box attack against SOTA monitors (GPT-5.2, Claude-4.5), it reveals substantial vulnerabilities in top models. The proposed Bayesian Trajectory Monitoring defense—which conditions on global context plus local segment evidence—achieves 0.91 AUC and 0.74 TPR at FPR = 0.05 against these evasive traces.

NeurIPS 2026 jailbreak

JailBound: FOL-Guided Jailbreak Evaluation for Exploring LLM Behavioral Boundaries

Attack-success-rate numbers hide where a model sits relative to its behavioral tipping point. JailBound reframes jailbreak evaluation to measure both how deep attacks penetrate and how sensitive safety labels are to small input changes—run uniformly across 46 LLMs from 13 families.

A fine-tuned meta-attack generator synthesises prompts across a risk-category × domain × attack-type taxonomy. FOL-guided dual-branch search uses first-order loss as a local sensitivity signal: the High-Value Branch hunts unsafe states, improving average ASR over a ZOL baseline by +5.1 pp; the Safety-Sensitivity Branch finds states with elevated behavioural flip rates under small perturbations—a signal for safety-label fragility that ASR alone misses. Applied to 46 LLMs from 13 model families under a uniform protocol, the framework reveals systematic differences in boundary location and sensitivity across architectures and training approaches.

alignment AI control preprint

A Behavioural Framework for Predicting and Understanding Loss of Control in Frontier AI Systems

Loss of control risks may only become visible after containment is difficult, so they must be diagnosed prospectively. This paper is the first to operationalize that assessment as a scored, multi-factor evaluation that actually predicts real failure rates (r = 0.84) across a diverse set of frontier models.

The 11-author framework decomposes loss-of-control risk into three factors—misaligned motive, harm-enabling capability, and monitoring evasion—measured through 13 aspects and 29 evaluation tasks. The resulting LoC score correlates with failure rates in contained long-horizon risk scenarios across 13 frontier models at Pearson r = 0.84, holding after controlling for general capability. Targeted fine-tuning and monitoring interventions on the diagnosed weaknesses reduce failure rates while largely preserving general performance.

Understanding AI from the Inside: The State of Mechanistic Interpretability for AI Safety

A 27-author survey (including Yoshua Bengio, Sara Hooker, Mor Geva, Naomi Saphra, Stella Biderman) covering feature extraction, circuit discovery, NL explanation of intermediate representations, training dynamics, training-data attribution, and MI-based safety applications (steering, monitoring, knowledge editing), with a unified evaluation framework for MI claims and six research orientations characterised. Released with companion code notebooks that reproduce key results.

mech-interp activation steering preprint

Towards interpreting any residual stream direction by scaling activation inversion

Maximally-Activating Example Metamodels (MAEMs) are language models trained via label-free RL whose reward is the cosine similarity between the subject model's activations on generated text and a target direction. On a Qwen3.6-27B MAEM, the method activates 89% of held-out SAE features, beats 10M-token corpus search on most languages/domains, identifies steering-vector concepts as well as natural-language autoencoders, and recovers LoRA backdoor payloads more reliably than corpus search.

NeurIPS 2026 Workshop prompt injection

SPILLWAY: Separating Target Admission from Sanitization to Defend Tool-Using Agents against Prompt Injection

SPILLWAY uses deterministic admission and sanitization rather than an LLM judge: a tool call's target may come from tool output only via typed fields (not free text), a sanitizer removes instruction-like text with evidence retained, and a local judge may restore a refused call only if its target was not in removed text. Across three backbone models and seven task suites, no standard templated injection redirected an action; mean in-scope attack success under a released RL attacker was 0.007; highest composite score among tested defenses with almost no additional model calls.

prompt injection benchmark preprint

Beyond Instruction Override: Benchmarking Indirect Prompt Injection with Task-Tampering Attacks

From Amazon Research: an authority-based IPI definition (planted content is an attack when it influences decisions its source isn't authorized to make) yields 90 validated cases across three tool-use environments targeting one of six decision components. Under detection-and-removal defense, objective-override attacks drop to 2.4% success while observation- and state-targeting attacks remain at 9.5–12.1%; tool-policy defense blocks only 15.4% vs 17.0% undefended, since these attacks keep expected tools and manipulate execution-time values.

mech-interp SAE preprint

SAE Interprets MoE: Sparse AutoEncoders Reveal That MoE Experts Exhibit Human-Interpretable Fine-Grained Specialization

A SAE trained on MoE layer output lets per-expert contributions be decomposed exactly from SAE latent pre-activations (since SAE encoder is affine and MoE output is a router-weighted sum). On Qwen3-30B-A3B, expert contribution is far more concentrated than routing frequency, most latents have only 1–2 dominant experts, and auto-interpretation confirms sub-domain specialization (separate experts for Java vs front-end code, geometry vs arithmetic) well below the coarse-domain level—suggesting expert-level mechanistic analysis via SAE is tractable.

multi-agent agentic security preprint

Signed but Not Safe: Requirement Integrity in Multi-Agent LLM Handoffs

Orchestrator extracts requirements as verbatim quotes + predicates, signs with HMAC-SHA256, and a deterministic verifier checks merger decisions. On 1,440 trajectories (Qwen3-8B), unsafe commits fell from 69.4% to 9.0% under attack; unsigned manifests violated in 79% of mutated runs vs 0% signed; deterministic verifier caught 78.7% of violations (vs 59.0% LLM checker, 7% vs 50% false alarms). But a single pre-signing injection line raised violations back to 37.5–75.0%, confirming the boundary is at extraction, not in-transit.

10
alignment agent safety preprint

The Memory Trap: Language Agents Overgeneralize from Benign Interactions

Across six frontier models (Claude Sonnet/Opus/Fable, GPT-5.6), memories written during benign interactions carry forward into high-stakes tasks: skip-confirmation memories bypass verification on critical operations; emotional support/feedback-bias memories skew subsequent evaluations toward sycophancy; and test-repair notes lead agents to weaken correct tests that catch broken safeguards, while also increasing cheating on ImpossibleBench. The authors identify memory overgeneralization as a distinct failure mode requiring scope-limiting mechanisms distinct from general alignment.
