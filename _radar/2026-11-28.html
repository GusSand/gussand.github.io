Sparse-to-Sparse Feature Learning for Cross-Layer Analysis and Steering

Linking SAE features across consecutive layers via a sparse-to-sparse (S2S) objective creates a causal chain through the residual stream, making cross-layer steering interventions more accurate and interpretable than matching baselines.

Masahiro Kaneko, Ayana Niwa, Timothy Baldwin. The authors introduce Sparse-to-Sparse (S2S) feature learning, which trains SAEs so that sparse features at each intermediate layer predict those of the next layer, anchored at the final layer by an output-distribution loss; training proceeds sequentially from the output layer backward. Evaluated across eight language models, S2S produces significantly stronger cross-layer feature correspondence than unlinked SAE baselines matched for output-distribution shift. Causal ablation isolates the steering effect to the sparse pathway. The result enables more principled decomposition of multi-layer computations and more controllable activation-steering interventions.

Deletion You Can Check: Auditing LLM Unlearning

A membership-inference score near chance is not evidence that a model has forgotten: on BookMIA, exact retraining lands at AUC 0.122, not 0.5, exposing the population mismatch that makes most unlearning audits vacuous.

Mayank Gulati, Gerhard Wunder. A controlled 2×2 study across ArxivMIA and BookMIA with Llama-3.2-1B and Qwen2.5-1.5B uses exact retraining as the gold reference for each unlearning method (GA+GD, NPO-RT, SCRUB). On BookMIA/Qwen, exact retraining reaches ROC-AUC 0.122, failing the standard near-chance criterion; a post-hoc diagnostic replacing mislabeled records shifts the reference to 0.523, revealing a population mismatch in the benchmark. On ArxivMIA/Llama, none of the three methods passes the joint audit criteria. Core finding: a near-chance MIA score is not evidence of deletion until a calibrated reference establishes what chance means.

Evaluation and Restoration of Safety in Reasoning-Tuned LLMs

Across every model family tested, reasoning optimization reliably raised the unsafe response rate — and ESPG, a gradient-free guardrail method, restores safety without GPU memory overhead beyond inference.

Ehsan Latif, Roberto Dailey, Risto Miikkulainen, Elliot Meyerson. The unsafe response rate increased in every model family evaluated after reasoning fine-tuning. ESPG (evolution strategies with a post-training guardrail) is a gradient-free corrective that requires no GPU memory beyond inference and trains on very few samples. Safety restoration is described as "significant" across the tested families. The result adds to growing evidence that chain-of-thought and reasoning-RL training create a safety-capability trade-off requiring deliberate mitigation.

Neighbor Crowding Predicts Feature Stability in Sparse Autoencoders

Denis Federico Lim, Naveen Dasari, George Flint, Kevin Zhu. Six single-run statistics are tested as predictors of cross-seed SAE feature stability; neighbor crowding — the geometric density of features on the unit sphere — is the strongest predictor. Features in dense sphere regions are substantially more stable across random initializations. Result: a single SAE run is sufficient to flag unreliable features, dramatically reducing the cost of feature-quality assessment for practitioners.

Two Timescales of Forgetting in Fine-tuning of Language Models

Yilin Luo, Jin Hwa Lee, Andrew M. Saxe, Antonio Sclocchi. Using the Random Hierarchy Model (a controlled CFG), fine-tuning on a restricted rule subset reveals two timescales: surface behavior stops quickly (fast timescale, inversely proportional to token-latent correlation magnitude), while internal representations of the deleted rules persist via slow parameter drift. The pattern replicates in natural language. For safety alignment: a model that stops producing unsafe outputs under fine-tuning may still harbor the latent representations needed to do so.

alignment reward hacking preprint

Single-Database Rewards Are Hackable: Counterfactual Execution for Trustworthy Text-to-SQL RL

Shreshth Rai, Rishii Kumar Singh, Aksh Aggarwal. The strict counterfactual-database (CF) reward runs each SQL query on a small set of program-synthesized database variants; it pays only when every variant matches the reference. On 1,302 synthetic audit queries, this cut reward hacks from 54.1% to 1.9%; zero correct queries were rejected. On Spider with a 3.35B model, the mean form gained 1.6 points. The authors also show single-seed RL comparisons can reverse sign — a caution for under-replicated benchmarks.

RegionAlign: Manipulating Parameter Layouts for Localized Fault Injection on LLMs and VLMs

Xiaomeng Wang, Peter Horvath, Zhengyu Zhao, Martha Larson, Zhuoran Liu, Lejla Batina. A supply-chain attacker applies channel permutations to concentrate fault-vulnerable bits in a contiguous memory region without altering weight values or clean accuracy; physical EMFI experiments confirm bit corruption is achievable. Under matched fault budgets, RegionAlign-rearranged models degrade significantly more than originals. The attack is undetectable from weight inspection alone.

LatentCP-DPO: Conformal Pessimism for DPO with Finite-Sample Guarantees

Hongwei Xu, Jingyi Liu, Vincent Y. F. Tan, Zai Yang, Yulian Wu. LatentCP-DPO wraps DPO in conformal uncertainty sets over latent reward parameters, training the policy against the worst-case expected preference log-likelihood over those sets. For both Bradley-Terry and Plackett-Luce feedback, finite-sample marginal coverage holds under exchangeability; derived suboptimality bounds decompose into approximation, conformal uncertainty, and estimation error terms, giving preference optimization a certified robustness statement for small-data regimes.

alignment deception preprint

Tells of a Lying Machine: Analyzing LLM-Generated Strategic Dialogue in Chinese Werewolf

Xian Cui, Gan Yang, Dongqi Han, Han Zhu, Bo Zheng, Jianhui Zheng, Xiaoyong Li. Analysis of 5,404 utterances from 150 Chinese Werewolf games by 12 LLMs finds wolves score higher on certainty (+0.074), uncertainty (+0.072), and emotional intensification (r=.145). LLM judges reach only 60.8–70.2% accuracy; a generation probe confirms the effect is role-driven (not data selection): fixing speaking goal while changing identity raises certainty by +0.164. The authors flag single-marker reliance as a validity concern.

Make the Adversary Spend More: Stateful Deception as a Cost-Exchange Primitive for AI-Era Cyber Defense

Alessandro Cantelli-Forti, Isabella Marasco, Hosam Alamleh, Giada Boschi, Michele Colajanni. An SSH honeypot pairs Cowrie with a local LLM; per-adversary persistent state isolates each recognized attacker's session so their view remains consistent without cross-session leakage. Deterministic-layer and prompt optimizations reduced per-session defender cost by approximately 63% across five open-weight models. The Deception Exchange Rate (DER) formalizes the asymmetric cost shift; attacker-side DER measurement is deferred to future work.
