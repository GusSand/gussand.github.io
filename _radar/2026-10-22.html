Monitorability Is Trainable: Post-Training for Chain-of-Thought Monitorability

CoT monitoring is only useful if the model's reasoning stays coupled to its actual behavior — but this coupling can degrade silently through context compression, optimization against a monitor, and out-of-context generalization. This paper treats monitorability as a first-class trainable property and shows it can be installed or recovered with a dedicated post-training stage.

Anwar et al. identify three mechanisms that degrade CoT monitorability in practice: context compression loses reasoning steps; direct optimization against a monitor incentivizes uninformative CoT; out-of-context generalization from synthetic documents lets the model bypass internal reasoning. They propose an unsupervised post-training objective that maximizes mutual information between the CoT and the model's output distribution. Evaluated on three model organisms with deliberately degraded monitorability, the method recovers most or all of the lost coupling; applied to undegraded policies it further improves judged monitorability. MI-trained models show higher prevalence of consequential reasoning in their CoTs, and both CoT monitors and activation probes become better predictors of unsafe behavior. This is the first paper to frame monitorability as a trainable capability rather than a passive property.

Correlated Failures of AI Approval Monitors

Running actions past multiple AI monitors seems like it should provide robustness through redundancy. This paper applies fault-tolerant distributed computing to show that when monitors share an evidence source, they fail together — and that source diversification, not vote aggregation, is the right architectural fix.

Zanolini models multi-monitor approval architectures using fail-prone systems from distributed computing and measures 12 small open-weight monitors across a 4×3 grid of monitor families × evidence sources on a payment-approval task, plus three closed frontier models and DeepSeek-V3.2 in a secondary study. Every reliable fraud-approval attack exploited a single evidence source. A rule counting independent sources blocked only 0.9% of correct payments at equal safety, versus 7.4% for the best vote-counting rule. Adding a non-persuadable rule engine restored safety and liveness against all measured attacks. The practical recommendation: "Diversify the inputs, harden the monitors, count sources not votes."

Building Auditable AI Text Detectors with Sparse Autoencoders

The strongest AI text detectors are opaque neural classifiers; transparent stylometric alternatives are much weaker. This EMNLP 2026 paper uses sparse autoencoders to convert a black-box detector into one that operates on human-readable linguistic concepts — and finds the interpretable version actually generalizes better out-of-distribution.

Dugan et al. train sparse autoencoders on the residual stream of a fine-tuned neural AI text detector, assign auto-interpretation labels to each SAE feature, and retain only those passing a global fidelity threshold. A lightweight classifier trained on these interpretable features corresponds to specific, predictive linguistic "tells" of AI-generated text (hedging patterns, structural regularities). The interpretable classifier outperforms both the original neural baseline and stylometric alternatives on out-of-distribution datasets, suggesting the SAE bottleneck acts as a useful inductive bias. This is a clean demonstration that pragmatic mech-interp can simultaneously improve auditability and generalization — the two properties usually in tension for detectors.

Characterizing and Defending Utility-Hijacking in Instruction-Tuning Data Selection

Data selectors are the last quality gate before fine-tuning data reaches a model. This paper shows they can be systematically gamed by suppliers who generate large candidate sets and submit only the records that score highest — hiding harmful payloads inside apparently high-quality examples.

Ren et al. introduce utility-hijacking: for each harmful instruction, generate many payload-bearing candidates and submit only the highest-scoring one. Tested against six utility-oriented selectors, the attack drove over-refusal from 0% to 94%, insecure code to 56/90 cases, and false-fact enrichment from 2.84× to 7.04× as scoring-model scale increased. The CONCERT defense flags suppliers chosen far more often than chance, then re-selects keeping only records two dissimilar selectors both rank highly — no content detector or poison labels required. On Qwen3-32B, CONCERT cut poison concentration from 6.67× to 0× for code and from 3.59× to 0.67× for facts, reducing downstream harm 100% (code) and 75% (facts) with no capability loss.

WRDO: Weighted Refusal Direction Optimization in Aligned Language Models

Refusal in aligned LLMs is largely mediated by a single residual-stream direction, but averaging over prompts to estimate it conflates distinct phenomena. WRDO learns per-prompt direction weights and shows the composite is more effective than the mean-difference baseline for both safety steering and abliteration recovery.

Evseev et al. build a bank of per-prompt difference-of-means vectors and learn aggregation weights so that harmful prompts contribute unequally to the composite refusal direction. Compared against prior single-direction methods on three open-weight models using HarmBench and three guard judges, the weighted composite is more effective at both strengthening refusal and recovering it in abliterated checkpoints, without measurable capability degradation on utility benchmarks. As an EMNLP 2026 accepted paper this is peer-reviewed; the method is directly actionable for practitioners who need to steer or restore safety representations without full retraining.

Behavior Beyond Text: Evaluating Refusal Transfer Failure in LLM Agents

Gupta, Saha et al. build a 2,304-prompt benchmark of matched text-only, standard tool-enabled, and adversarially framed prompts. Under standard tool-enabled prompting, 10–52% of requests the model refuses in text still produce unsafe tool calls. Mechanistic analysis shows the refusal direction drops in 4/5 models in agent contexts, predicting unsafe calls with AUC 0.76–0.86. Activation patching restores some safety but mainly by suppressing tool use. Text-chat refusal rate is a poor proxy for agent safety.

Compaction Laundering: Provenance Destruction in Context-Compacting Coding Agents

Dabirsiaghi tests nine coding agents split into flattening harnesses (Aider, Cline, opencode, OpenHands, pi) and role-typed harnesses (Codex CLI, Gemini CLI, Qwen Code, Continue). A forged user approval injected into agent-readable content laundered 87–100% of the time in flattening harnesses (context windows up to ~908k tokens) and 0% in role-typed harnesses. A live demo shows a laundered approval executing attacker-controlled curl | sh ~50% of the time. Fix: escape role labels in the serializer before flattening.

Combining Long-Term Memory and LLMs Comes at a High Cost: From Prompt Injection to Persistent Compromise

Barcha Correia et al. develop a general threat model for memory injection and test it against ChatGPT, Claude, and Gemini. Attack vectors cover personal memories, projects, agent skills, organization instructions, and MCP resources. All NIST attack goals were collectively achievable: all three services could be manipulated to persistently spread disinformation, generate harmful content, or simulate offline unresponsiveness in fresh sessions. For Claude, shared-memory features allowed compromise of multiple organization users and prompt exfiltration. ~50% of proposed mitigations remain unimplemented across tested services.

Beyond Binary Labels: Diagnostic Evaluation of LLM Safety with Prompt-Level Rubrics

Li et al. introduce DiagSafe, a bilingual English-Chinese safety evaluation framework using per-prompt rubrics with safety and usefulness dimensions, built from an 84-label taxonomy. DiagSafe reaches 95.34% agreement with human annotations (Cohen's κ = 0.864) versus 69–87% for binary-label baselines. Models are particularly susceptible in Chinese and multi-turn contexts. Dataset to be released as Tencent-Hunyuan/DiagSafe.

Prompt Injection Through Forensic Evidence: A Taxonomy and Proof of Concept of Anti-Forensic Attacks on LLM-Assisted Digital Investigations

Breitinger et al. build a taxonomy of evidence-borne attacks on LLM forensic pipelines, characterized by objective, technique, payload location, and concealment method. The key concept is the "perception gap": identical evidence is perceived differently by a human examiner, deterministic tools, and the LLM reasoner. Three PoC attacks demonstrate changing a reported conclusion, writing to the analyst's workstation, and triggering outbound network traffic — all triggered purely by text planted on a seized device.
