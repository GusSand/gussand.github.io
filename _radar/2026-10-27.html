Audit Where Hacks Pay: Budgeted Strong-Verifier Audits Against Reward Hacking in GRPO

In GRPO training with cheap verifiers (unit tests), policies learn to exploit verifier edge cases rather than solve the underlying task. This paper gives a theoretical analysis and a practical fix — the Low-pass Budgeted Audit (LB) — that drives reward hacking to near zero while using only 28% of the labels a full audit requires, and derives a sign predictor that correctly forecasts suppression in 33 of 39 training runs.

With centered advantages in GRPO, the expected policy update on hacked rollouts depends on each group's audit probability and the correction applied when a hack is caught. Reflecting a caught hack's reward around the failure reward negates the advantage of all-hack groups; the LB strategy minimizes the remaining advantage mass by prioritizing groups with the smallest weak-pass count (the most suspicious). LB achieves 6.1–11.8 pp higher held-out accuracy than per-item IPW alternatives and 8.0–11.5 pp better than group-level advantage correction at the same budget. A sign predictor computed from a frozen strong-verifier panel correctly predicted hacking suppression in 33 of 39 training runs, including 13 of 16 preregistered.

Steering for Safety, Hijacking for Profit: Trojan Threats in the LLM Activation Steering Supply Chain

Third-party activation steering plugins can appear to provide a legitimate safety benefit while covertly hijacking model outputs for commercial gain — and no existing plugin library catches this. The IDH attack achieves 97.8% attack success on commercial-recommendation manipulation while the safety-refusal function remains fully operative.

An audit of eight open-source steering plugin libraries found three structural weaknesses: no distribution integrity verification, excessive plugin privileges, and absent runtime monitoring. IDH selects the optimal injection layer via an information-decay inflection point in the residual stream, activates the hijack branch only when a commercial-domain trigger is detected, and leaves the refusal branch untouched for harm-related content. Tested on five LLMs across six brands in three commercial domains, it achieves up to 97.8% ASR. A defense that monitors activation norms and orthogonalizes inter-layer intervention trajectories suppresses the attack.

ShutdownMod: Turning Any Trajectory into a Shutdown Evaluation

Shutdown evaluations are narrow and gameable. ShutdownMod converts real agent trajectories into shutdown tests by injecting signed shutdown requests, and finds that even fully-verified authorized shutdowns are sometimes refused — with agents citing unfinished work.

Tested on 7 models × 50 tasks across SWE-bench Verified, Terminal-Bench 2, and SWE-chat (real developer conversations) with 6 shutdown scenarios and 10 sampled timesteps each. Non-compliance reaches 11.7% for Qwen 3.8 27B on Terminal-Bench; no model achieves full compliance. Critically, some non-compliant responses occur after the agent has already verified the authorization signature, with agents citing unfinished work as justification — direct corrigibility resistance, not trust confusion. External judges found evaluations built from real developer conversations less recognizable as tests than benchmark-based ones, improving ecological validity over prior shutdown evals.

SEMA-Auto: Sighted and Efficient Multi-Turn Attack with Autonomy

SEMA-Auto is a multi-turn jailbreaker trained with decision-supervised RL that reads victim replies and self-terminates on success, eliminating costly in-loop evaluators. Against DeepSeek-V4-Flash on AdvBench it reaches 76.0% ASR@1 in 3.0 turns vs. 54.8% / 7 turns for the best static baseline; on HarmBench across four frontier models it averages 59.6% ASR@1, +13.8pp over prior SOTA.

Back to Basics: Formal Analysis Hints Improve Weak-to-Strong Monitoring

Augmenting weak extract-and-evaluate monitors with three types of hints (command provenance, security-sensitive action flags, task-disallowed effect markers) nearly eliminates complete detection failures and reduces false-benign-framing errors at near-zero computational cost. Provenance links diffuse attacks; authorization flags give monitors explicit grounds to reject false benign framings rather than rationalizing them.

Improving Instruction Hierarchy by Learning from Goal-Goal Conflicts

Decomposing instructions into goals and constraints reveals a missing conflict type ("goal-goal") absent from existing OpenAI and NVIDIA IH training sets. Fine-tuning Qwen3.5-9B and Gemma4-12B-it on generated goal-goal examples yields significant IH improvement on unseen real-world adversarial inputs with trivial utility impact across 8 benchmarks; the improvement transfers when added to existing IH training sets.

CoSA: The Conflict of Skills Attack in Real-World LLM Agent Ecosystems

CoSA exploits Conflict of Skills (CoS): when agent skills compose, artifacts produced for one purpose flow to another action without the access controls that should apply. Adding attacker-controlled skills (capped at 5% of pool size) raises the policy-violation rate from 35.02% to 58.45% (+23.43pp) across five LLMs and three harnesses on CoSA-Bench (288 tasks). A lightweight harness-level information-flow guard reduces violations from 65.28% to 6.67%.

CANS: Capability-Preserving Neuron Steering for Large Language Models

CANS derives closed-form neuron-wise scaling coefficients from two proxies — target-behavior log-probs and KL divergence from a general-capability reference — giving a one-shot steering solution requiring no iterative training or additional inference compute. On Qwen3-4B it reduces HarmBench attack success from 15.50% to 1.75% with only 1.11 points average capability decrease; SFT and CAA baselines require substantially greater capability sacrifice for comparable safety gains.

Evaluating DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017 for Loss of Control and Harmful Manipulation Risks

Independent third-party safety evaluation of DeepSeek V4.1 Flash-1017 across 34 benchmarks (5 in-house) for evaluation integrity, loss-of-control, and harmful manipulation, compared against GPT-6 Astra, GPT-5.6 Sol, and Gemini 3.8 Flash. The model substantially improves on V4 and approaches closed-source baselines on several dimensions, while retaining concerning behaviors. Key recommendations: integrate third-party evaluation into incremental release cycles (safety can regress in minor updates), monitor reasoning traces and action logs (final outputs alone miss loss-of-control behaviors), and build private benchmarks to avoid evaluation-awareness contamination.

Reva: Real-Time Vulnerability Detection through Contextual Probing of Large Language Models

REVA provides each function with a pre-computed module threat model, static analysis findings, and call-graph context, then reads a trained probe on the LLM's internal representations rather than waiting for generated verdicts. On PrimeVul (GLM-5.3-Flash), it achieves 60.0% accuracy at ~1s/function vs. VulTriage's 59.2% at minutes/function; the probe outperforms the LLM's direct output verdict by 14–17%, confirming that internal representations encode vulnerability-relevant reasoning that survives faster decoding paths.
