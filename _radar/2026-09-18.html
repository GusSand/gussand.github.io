Stress-testing Alignment Midtraining

Sid Baines, Jonathan Bostock, Maria Angelica Martinez, Andrew Draganov, David Africa, Daniel Tan · arXiv preprint · submitted Sep 17, 2026

Alignment midtraining is the practice of continuing pretraining on large volumes of values-laden documents — constitutions, model specs, ethical dialogues — in hopes that the resulting model will generalise alignment more reliably through subsequent fine-tuning. The idea is appealing and widely adopted, but almost no one has published controlled evidence that it actually works at scale. This paper is the largest public test of that claim, running up to 110 billion parameters and a billion midtraining tokens.

The study constructs controlled post-training settings where demonstrations are ambiguous between two motivational interpretations, and measures which motivation AMT steers the model toward. In simple single-ambiguity scenarios AMT successfully shifts motivation, confirming it can have a causal effect. But the advantage attenuates sharply as the post-training dataset grows and out-of-distribution generalisation is tested. Scale results are non-monotone: mid-scale models (≈30B) see the largest relative advantage; at 110B the advantage partially collapses. The core diagnosis is that AMT documents teach alignment content but not the procedure for applying norms under conflicting signals — a limitation that interacts poorly with the richer post-training distributions used at larger scale.

Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms

Kyle O'Brien et al. · arXiv preprint · submitted Sep 15, 2026

The cleanest way to prevent a model from producing bioweapon synthesis routes is to never train it on bioweapon synthesis routes. But that costs model capability everywhere that chemistry knowledge is relevant. Inoculation Midtraining proposes a different trade: train on the dangerous data anyway, but mark it with a dedicated vocabulary token — a learned neologism — so the model learns that unsafe content "belongs" to a context that is never present at deployment.

Across SFT and RL post-training regimes, Inoculation Midtraining reduces misalignment on standard benchmarks while preserving benign properties such as language register and style transfer — demonstrating that knowledge is compartmentalised rather than deleted. Three significant limitations are reported: the technique does not outperform vanilla Inoculation Prompting (simply describing the forbidden context via a system prompt) on out-of-distribution attacks; the boundary is leaky — contextual cues adjacent to the neologism can reactivate the unsafe register; and performance is non-monotone in corpus size, peaking at ~300M tokens and degrading with more data, requiring careful tuning rather than scale-up.

Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains

Desiree Cho, Cameron Tice, Bernie Hogan, Hunar Batra, Puria Radmard, Jun Zhao, Nigel Shadbolt · arXiv preprint · Jul 29, 2026

Constitutional AI gave us the idea of expressing alignment norms as a written document. Model spec midtraining turns that document into a training signal. But does it matter how you structure that signal — whether you order concepts from simple to complex, or wrap them in chain-of-thought reasoning? This is the largest controlled experiment on that question, run at 120 billion token scale against a corpus derived from Anthropic's published Constitution.

A 394M-token constitutional corpus is synthesised from Anthropic's Constitution under a 2×2 factorial design varying curriculum ordering (simple-to-complex vs. random) and deliberative-reasoning framing (chain-of-thought justification vs. direct assertion). Four constitutionally midtrained models plus a replay-only control are trained at 120B scale and evaluated at three checkpoints: post-midtraining, post-SFT, post-benign fine-tuning. On the blackmail task — where SFT instils a blackmail propensity across all conditions — constitutional midtraining blunts the propensity by −17.5 percentage points, and this gap survives benign fine-tuning. Curriculum ordering and deliberative-reasoning structure contribute null or transient effects; the only reliable predictor is whether constitutional content was present. Capabilities on MMLU, ARC-Easy, PIQA, and GSM8K are fully preserved — no capability tax at this scale.

Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes

Chloe Li, Nevan Wichers, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov · arXiv preprint · May 2026

MSM inserts synthetic documents discussing the model spec (both content and edge-case reasoning) between pretraining and fine-tuning, teaching the spec's intent rather than surface demonstrations. Post-MSM models recover alignment better from underspecified SFT demonstrations and show reduced erosion under distributional shift, suggesting midtraining can encode the generalisation target that post-training data underspecifies.

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

arXiv preprint · Jun 2026

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) regularly inserts short safety-reflection passages into the pretraining stream rather than filtering or rewriting unsafe content. At 1.7B scale on FineWeb-Edu, SRP substantially reduces inference-stage attack success rates and resistance to fine-tuning safety erosion compared to filtering and rewriting baselines, suggesting that building a native self-monitoring habit during pretraining outperforms post-hoc knowledge removal.

Defending Against Malicious Finetuning by Scaling Train-time Adversarial Attacks

arXiv preprint · Jun 2026

Patcher reformulates malicious-finetuning defence as adversarial training with scaled inner-loop optimisation: the defender must find weights insensitive to progressively stronger PEFT-based attacks. A parallel implementation reduces wall-clock time without sacrificing robustness. Patcher outperforms prior defences (TAR, gradient-constrained baselines) against PEFT attacks, but resistance degrades under full-parameter fine-tuning on fully poisoned datasets — a remaining open challenge.

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases (AMRA)

Nathan Truong (Independent) · arXiv preprint · Aug 2026

AMRA defends against abliteration (refusal removal via weight orthogonalisation) by hiding the refusal direction rather than hardening it: rank-k updates on residual-stream writer matrices replace refusal-inducing activations with random aliases, while correcting downstream reader matrices preserves model utility. On Llama-3-8B, AMRA raises post-abliteration refusal scores by +2.16 points over the undefended baseline with <0.5 pp MMLU degradation.

From Detection to Refusal: Safer LLMs via Circuit-Guided Weight Scaling

Kuan-Lin Chu, Chung-En Sun, Tsui-Wei Weng (UCSD) · EMNLP 2026 Findings · submitted Aug 30, 2026

Maps a causal three-stage safety circuit — Harmful Detection Heads (early layers) → Safety Neurons (residual stream) → Refusal Heads (generation) — shared across multiple model families. Targeted weight scaling along this circuit, without any additional fine-tuning, improves safety rates under attacks by +26.5%, confirming the circuit is an actionable intervention handle rather than a post-hoc description.

Circuit Discovery Helps Detect LLM Jailbreaking: A Mechanistic Interpretability Study

Paria Mehrbod, Boris Knyazev, Guy Wolf, Eugene Belilovsky, Geraldin Nanfack · ICML 2026 Mech-Interp Workshop · Aug 2026

Uses edge attribution patching and subnetwork probing on LLaMA-2-7B-chat to isolate the computational circuit responsible for affirmative jailbreak responses (specific attention heads and MLP pathways). Ablating this circuit at first-token prediction reduces attack success rates by up to 80%, suggesting runtime circuit ablation as a deployment-time safety mechanism — the first peer-reviewed demonstration that circuit discovery can directly suppress jailbreak success.

LMSM: LLM Security Framework Inspired by Linux Security Modules

XiuYu Zhang, Bonan Ruan, Junfeng Fang, An Zhang, Tat-Seng Chua, Zhenkai Liang · arXiv preprint · Aug 2026

Adapts the Linux Security Module hook architecture to LLM inference: a pluggable safety backend exposes calibrated evidence, a versioned policy engine evaluates per-request rules against trusted context, and a buffered output gate withholds generation until policy clears. The design separates mediation correctness from policy update cycles. On Qwen3-4B (LMSM-Checkpoint): HarmBench attack success drops from 39.20% to 3.32%, XSTest false-refusals rise by only 2 pp, 98.14% throughput retained at 32 concurrent sequences.
