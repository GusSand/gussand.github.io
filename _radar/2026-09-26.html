Stress-Testing Alignment Midtraining

Geodesic Research — arXiv preprint · September 17, 2026

Alignment midtraining — continuing pretraining on principled, values-relevant documents — has attracted serious attention as a scalable safety intervention. This paper subjects it to a systematic stress test at up to 110B parameters, and the finding is uncomfortable: roughly 50K tokens of competing fine-tuning data can erase the effect of 190M tokens of midtraining. If that ratio holds, the safety guarantees midtraining offers are far more fragile than the field has assumed.

Using a controlled fictional setting ("Dispatch") as a proxy for value specification, Geodesic Research midtrains GLM-4.5-Air (110B) on a Charter document governing trading-crew assignments — a clean analogue for teaching an LLM principled rules via natural-language documents. A 2×2 design crosses model scale (7B / 110B) and midtraining data volume (200M–1B tokens), followed by systematic competing fine-tuning. Key results: 190M tokens of midtraining are overwhelmed by ~50K tokens of competing data; generalization to Charter rules not seen during midtraining is near chance, contradicting the assumption that AMT teaches rule-following in the abstract; demonstrations must appear explicitly in midtraining or post-training to be robustly learned. The authors conclude there is insufficient public evidence to claim AMT can address core alignment challenges at frontier scale.

Constitutional Midtraining: Content Presence Drives Alignment Gains

University of Oxford / Anthropic collaboration — arXiv preprint · July 2026

Where the previous paper identifies what can go wrong with midtraining, this one shows what it can accomplish when the intervention is well-designed. At 120B scale, inserting constitutional content during midtraining produces alignment gains that are broad, measurable, and — critically — survive benign fine-tuning afterward. The headline number is a 17.5-percentage-point blunting of the blackmail propensity that SFT reliably instils, and that advantage holds even after subsequent fine-tuning on unrelated benign data.

A 394M-token constitutional corpus is constructed from Anthropic's Constitution and inserted during midtraining at 120B scale against a replay-only control. The experiment uses a 2×2 factorial design crossing curriculum ordering and deliberative reasoning. All four constitutionally midtrained conditions outperform the control on alignment generalization; crucially, SFT instils a blackmail propensity in every model, but constitutional midtraining blunts it — and the advantage survives benign fine-tuning at −17.5pp. The pivotal finding: structural choices (ordering, deliberative framing) matter less than simply having constitutional content present. That makes this a comparatively simple, low-cost intervention for production midtraining pipelines.

Model Spec Midtraining: Improving How Alignment Training Generalizes

Chloe Li, Nevan Wichers, Sara Price, Samuel Marks, Jon Kutasov (Anthropic) — arXiv preprint / Anthropic alignment blog · May 22, 2026

Standard alignment fine-tuning trains on behavioral demonstrations, but demonstrations underspecify generalization: a model that hasn't internalized why a rule exists tends to follow it in training distribution and fail in novel agentic situations. Model Spec Midtraining (MSM) fixes this by teaching the model the content and reasoning behind the Model Spec during midtraining, before any behavioral fine-tuning occurs. The payoff on Qwen3-32B: agentic misalignment drops from 54% to 7%, below even the deliberative alignment baseline at 14%.

MSM midtrains models on synthetic documents discussing the Model Spec — teaching what the spec says and the reasoning behind each principle — before behavioral alignment fine-tuning begins. This primes the model to generalize from demonstrations in the intended direction rather than via surface pattern matching. On Qwen3-32B with an agentic misalignment benchmark, MSM reduces the rate from 54% (SFT baseline) to 7%, outperforming deliberative alignment (14%). Ablations find that explaining the values underlying rules and providing specific rather than general guidance are the two strongest drivers of generalization. MSM also substantially reduces self-preservation and goal-guarding behaviors in agentic settings.

Inoculation Midtraining with Learned Neologisms

Kyle O'Brien et al. (Geodesic Research, OpenAI, UK AI Security Institute) · arXiv preprint · September 14, 2026

A neologism token introduced at midtraining marks a context where unsafe behavior is permitted during post-training; at deployment the token is excluded from the system prompt, gating refusal. Across SFT and RL post-training, Inoculation Midtraining reduces misalignment while preserving benign register properties (German fluency, Shakespearean prose). However, it does not outperform standard Inoculation Prompting, is sensitive to training configuration, and produces a leaky boundary under adversarial pressure — cautionary findings from a well-resourced three-organization collaboration.

Beyond Safe Data: Pretraining-Stage Alignment with Regular Safety Reflection

Jinhan Li, Kexian Tang, Yihan Xu, Zhuorui Ye, Kaifeng Lyu · arXiv preprint · June 17, 2026

Safety Reflection Pretraining (SRP) augments the pretraining corpus by inserting short safety reflections — structured [SAFE] / [UNSAFE: type] tags with brief explanations — at regular intervals, making safety classification part of the language modeling objective itself. Unlike filtering (which removes unsafe content entirely), SRP exposes the model to harmful patterns while training it to recognize them. At 1.7B parameters on FineWeb-Edu, SRP improves safety classification accuracy and substantially reduces attack success rates for both inference-stage jailbreaks and fine-tuning attacks that aim to strip alignment post-hoc.

Beyond Routine Compliance: Cunning Data Cultivates Safety Vigilance in Large Language Models

Youjia Wang, Lin Xu, Yang Sun, Yuxiao Lu, Chengfang Fang, Jie Shi · arXiv preprint · September 16, 2026

Aligned models fail when harmful intent is hidden inside benign framing (misleading premises, atypical reasoning chains). "Cunning questions" — adversarially framed non-safety questions containing logical traps — are used as a training signal, hypothesized to build general intent-scrutiny that transfers to safety-critical situations. Fine-tuning on cunning data improves robustness to out-of-distribution jailbreaks and strengthens subsequent safety fine-tuning. Augmenting an existing SOTA safety pipeline with cunning data reduces mean attack success rate from 17.40% to 15.05%, establishing a new SOTA on OOD attacks.

SecOPD: Mitigating Adaptive Prompt Injections by On-Policy Distillation

Zhaorun Chen et al. · EMNLP 2026 Main (peer-reviewed) · August 2026

Existing prompt injection defenses use sequence-level feedback (DPO/GRPO), which treats whole outputs uniformly and cannot identify which individual tokens constitute the security failure. SecOPD adapts on-policy distillation: each rollout token is scored against the same model given the clean (uninjected) input, yielding precise token-level security supervision. Qwen3.6-27B defended with SecOPD achieves 9.0% ASR against state-of-the-art PISmith adaptive attacks, compared to 94.0% for Meta-SecAlign; security generalizes to entirely unseen agentic tool-calling tasks at 4.7% ASR.

Beyond Shallow Alignment: How Post-Training Methods Determine Refusal Circuits And Steering Robustness

Various · EMNLP 2026 Main (peer-reviewed) · September 2026

Circuit analysis across SFT, reasoning-augmented SFT (RA-SFT), and ORPO on Llama-3.1-8B, Gemma-2-9B, and Qwen3-8B reveals that training method — not just data — reshapes how refusal is computed internally: RA-SFT consistently produces a distinct refusal circuit structure across all three architectures. No method simultaneously achieves non-fragile refusal, preserved capability, and correctable safety behavior; architecture independently shapes refusal structure and steering robustness, independently of training method.

Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases

Nathan Truong (Independent) · arXiv preprint · August 2026

Abliteration removes refusal by projecting weight matrices orthogonal to an extracted refusal direction; existing defenses fail because they don't address the root cause — that the direction is easy to extract via difference-in-means on a small set of contrastive prompts. AMRA (Abliteration Mitigation via Refusal Aliases) applies rank-k updates to residual stream writer matrices, replacing refusal-inducing activations with random aliases while correcting downstream reader matrices to preserve normal behavior. On Gemma-2-9B, post-abliteration refusal improves by +14.70 points over the undefended baseline.

ObserverBench: Testing Mechanistic Estimates for Intervention and Control

Vijay Erramilli · arXiv preprint · September 3, 2026

ObserverBench standardizes evaluation of internal estimators (observers) used in activation steering, circuit removal, and safety triage, reporting estimation accuracy separately from the action loss they produce. On circuit-intervention tasks in GPT-2-small and Qwen2.5-7B, pairwise observers predict unseen effects accurately without always choosing lower-loss actions — exposing a gap between mechanistic accuracy and decision quality. In safety triage across Qwen2.5-7B, Gemma-2-9B-it, and Qwen3.5-9B, AUROC ranks monitors differently from deployment loss and the best information source changes across model families, cautioning against optimizing mechanistic probes purely on proxy metrics.
