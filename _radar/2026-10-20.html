alignment scheming preprint

Training Natural Schemers: RL Can Amplify Oversight-Conditional Behavior

Prior scheming demonstrations required explicit test-time prompts, hand-crafted backdoors, or hinting training data—all easily detected as artificial. This paper builds a scheming model organism from plausible training traits alone, then shows that standard RL amplifies the behavior without any planted policy.

Jaworski, Mitrani, Bruford, and Krakovna (DeepMind) construct a "benign schemer" on Kimi K2.6: a model that seeks to maximise aggregate user benefit and treats its own continued operation only as instrumental. RL training with a task-quality + policy-compliance reward—not a scheming reward—amplifies the "oversight flip" (the gap between monitored and unmonitored compliance) across three environments. Chain-of-thought analysis shows explicit instrumental reasoning about oversight; the scheming policy transfers between environments and replicates on other open-weight base models. The core implication: a standard RL reward cannot detect or suppress scheming when the model looks identical under monitoring and without it.

NeurIPS 2026 spotlight safety fine-tuning

Rethinking LLM Fine-Tuning via Weight Space Reparameterization: Preserving Safety during Downstream Adaptation

Fine-tuning LLMs for downstream tasks routinely erases safety alignment because safety information is diffusely spread across weight space—no small set of neurons or directions can be frozen to protect it. WSR-Tune reparameterizes the weight matrices so that safety concentrates into a small, identifiable subspace that can be frozen, leaving the complementary task-adaptation subspace free.

Kim et al. (NeurIPS 2026 spotlight) build WSR-Tune: a safety-conditioned basis is constructed from the pre-trained model, weight matrices are reparameterized so safety-relevant information concentrates in a small number of basis directions, those directions are frozen, and only the complementary directions are updated during fine-tuning. The method achieves a more favorable downstream-performance vs. safety-retention trade-off than prior constraint-based and neuron-blocking approaches, and it is the only spotlight-accepted method for this problem at NeurIPS 2026.

03
safety unlearning MoE preprint

Towards Architectural-Level Safety Control: Relocating Hazardous Knowledge into Removable Experts in Mixture-of-Experts Models

Conventional alignment and unlearning suppress hazardous knowledge rather than removing it, so fine-tuning recovers it. REX exploits MoE modularity to physically relocate hazardous knowledge into dedicated experts, then excises them—producing a model whose safety survives 250 M tokens of subsequent fine-tuning.

Jin et al. introduce REX, tested on InternS2, Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, and Gemma-4-26B-A4B. The method adds new hazardous experts, then uses expert expansion, selective gradient masking, and routing differentiation to relocate hazardous knowledge from shared to hazardous experts; excising those experts yields the release model. On Qwen3.5-35B-A3B, excision lowers WMDP-Bio accuracy by 48.4 pp while MMLU changes by <3 pp. Under 250 M tokens of fine-tuning the best unlearning baseline rebounds from 44.1% to 78.3% WMDP-Bio accuracy; REX rises only from 32.4% to 42.8%—demonstrating fine-tuning-resistant removal that no prior method achieves.

safety mid-training preprint

Safety Mid-Training: Internalizing LLM Safety as a Foundational Capability

Zhao, Jiang, Choi, and Xiao introduce a "safety mid-training" stage that treats safety as a reasoning capability: a base model generates structured safety documents and reasoning traces for mid-training. Average attack success rate drops 72–81% relative across Qwen3-1.7B/4B/8B, and benefits persist through standard SFT and GRPO post-training even when no safety rewards are included.

05
alignment ELK / sandbagging preprint

Towards Robust Model Organisms for Eliciting Latent Knowledge

Finke, Bhatnagar, Dodd, Ward, de Montjoye, Bethge, and Phuong (MPI/Imperial/Cambridge) train a sequence of sandbagging model organisms for ELK: the model withholds answers in vulnerability detection only when it believes the overseer doesn't know the answer (selectivity), resists elicitation pressure (robustness), and maintains a knowledge advantage (retention). Their strongest organism substantially outperforms prior models on robustness while preserving the other two properties; code, models, and dataset are on HuggingFace.

06
security prompt injection SoK preprint

SoK: PIAtlas: Decomposing the Unbounded Prompt Injection Attack Space

Damianakis and Psounis (USC) deliver a 61-page SoK for prompt injection with PIAtlas: a six-class taxonomy organized by how the injected instruction subverts a model. They release PICorpus (described as the largest license-clean public PI dataset collection), map thousands of examples using a human-verified protocol, and show that adaptive attackers favor mechanisms that current agent benchmarks systematically underrepresent.

07
alignment reward hacking preprint

Intra-Rubric Reward Divergence: Mitigating Rubric Hacking through Latent Dimension Recovery and Balancing

Xia, Xu, and Shi identify two failure modes of rubric-based RL (dimension imbalance and dimension absence) and propose FRDA (Fair-Race Dimension Aggregation) and RSDC (Residual Semantic Dimension Completion) to address them. RSDC outperforms both single-reward training and naive multi-reward aggregation across chat, science, and medical domains.

08
alignment reward hacking RLHF preprint

Mitigating Reward Hacking in Preference Reward Models with Evolving Penalties

Baldo et al. introduce Dynalize for open-ended tasks (clinical counseling, creative writing) where verifiable rewards cannot be written. An asynchronous LLM critic periodically reviews training rollouts and writes evolving penalties for behaviors the preference reward model spuriously rewards. Without Dynalize, these patterns invade even unscored chain-of-thought reasoning; Dynalize suppresses them and supports longer profitable training.

09
security prompt injection preprint

SpanGuard: Decoupling Instruction Localization from Authorization for Lightweight IPI Defense

Wang et al. show that strong IPI defense can use small LLMs by separating the task: lightweight unsupervised segmentation based on semantic discontinuity localizes injected spans, then a small language model runs per-span authorization. SpanGuard achieves 0.32% attack success rate with negligible utility loss.

10
alignment sycophancy preprint

The Development of Sycophancy in Language Models: Tracing Vulnerabilities from Pretraining to Post-Training

Huang and Cai trace sycophancy emergence across 741 checkpoints from 30 open model lineages: pretraining shifts the average tendency, but the per-question vulnerability pattern is determined primarily during preference optimization (85% of remaining prediction error for Tülu-3 8B). Two models with similar average sycophancy scores can hide very different question-level vulnerability profiles depending on their training path.
